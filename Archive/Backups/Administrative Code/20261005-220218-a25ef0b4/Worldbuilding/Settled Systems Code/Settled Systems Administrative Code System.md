# Settled Systems Administrative Code

> [!summary]
> The **Settled Systems Administrative Code**, commonly shortened to the **Settled Systems Code**, is the shared legal and regulatory framework used throughout the [[Settled Systems]].
>
> Code citations use a machine-readable structure inherited from early intersystem standards databases:
>
> `A[TITLE]%[CHAPTER]&[SECTION]-[CLAUSE]`
>
> Example:
>
> `A2%13&1-3`
>
> This is read as **Administrative Title 2, Chapter 13, Section 1, Clause 3**.

# Instructions for ChatGPT

> [!important] Using This Note in Another Chat
> When writing, interpreting, or assigning Settled Systems Code citations:
>
> - Follow the citation structure and title register in this note.
> - Preserve all previously established code assignments.
> - Do not assign a new meaning to a code that already appears in the Existing Code Register.
> - Choose the Administrative Title according to the law’s primary legal effect.
> - Reuse an existing chapter when the new law fits its subject.
> - Create a new chapter only when no existing chapter is appropriate.
> - Treat chapter, section, and clause numbers as registry numbers rather than hidden symbolic codes.
> - Use sparse numbering when useful so later provisions can be inserted without renumbering older laws.
> - Distinguish between the law’s public justification, its statutory language, its administrative interpretation, and its practical effect.
> - Write official legal language in a calm, technical, institutionally neutral tone.
> - Allow the practical implementation of a law to be exploitative even when the statutory language sounds reasonable.
> - Erbium Industries should rarely describe coercion openly. Use terms such as stewardship, verification, continuity, public safety, infrastructure protection, service eligibility, and responsible administration.
> - Do not make every law secretly fraudulent. Some provisions should solve real problems while distributing their costs unfairly.
> - When a proposed law could reasonably belong under more than one title, identify the controlling provision and use cross-references for related enforcement, property, labor, or security rules.
> - Ask for clarification when the law’s actual purpose, enforcing authority, affected population, or intended consequences are unclear.

# Citation Structure

A complete citation contains four levels:

`A[TITLE]%[CHAPTER]&[SECTION]-[CLAUSE]`

## Administrative Title

The `A#` portion identifies the broad legal field.

Example:

`A2`

Administrative Title 2 governs personhood, ancestry, identity, biology, and embodiment.

The title number should always have a consistent meaning throughout the setting.

## Chapter

The `%#` portion identifies a major subject within the title.

Example:

`%13`

Chapter 13 of Administrative Title 2 governs synthetic persons and manufactured sapience.

Chapter numbers are permanent registry assignments. They do not need to appear in chronological or numerical order.

## Section

The `&#` portion identifies a specific regulatory area within the chapter.

Example:

`&1`

Section 1 of the synthetic-person chapter governs android assembly and reproduction.

## Clause

The `-#` portion identifies the specific enforceable rule.

Example:

`-3`

Clause 3 establishes the exclusive right of android communities to assemble new androids and prohibits non-android interests from manufacturing them.

# Reading a Citation

`A2%13&1-3`

Formal reading:

> Settled Systems Administrative Code, Administrative Title 2, Chapter 13, Section 1, Clause 3.

Common spoken reading:

> Title Two, Chapter Thirteen, Section One, Clause Three.

Common written reference:

> Settled Systems Code `A2%13&1-3`

# Optional Citation Extensions

Use these only when a law needs greater specificity.

## Subclauses

Letters identify subdivisions within a clause.

- `A2%1&4-13.a`
- `A2%1&4-13.b`
- `A2%1&4-13.c`

Example:

- `A2%1&4-13.a` — masking a continuity node
- `A2%1&4-13.b` — physical removal of a continuity node
- `A2%1&4-13.c` — transmission of falsified continuity credentials

A subclause remains part of the parent clause and should not be treated as an independent law.

## Multiple Citations

Use commas when citing distinct clauses:

`A2%1&4-13, A6%4&2-7`

Use two periods for a continuous range:

`A2%1&4-13..16`

This means Clauses 13 through 16.

Do not use a single hyphen to indicate a range. The hyphen already separates the section from the clause.

# Numbering Principles

## The Title Number Has Meaning

The Administrative Title identifies the law’s broad legal function.

A law governing wages belongs under `A4`.

A law governing body ownership belongs under `A3`.

A law governing legal personhood belongs under `A2`.

## Other Numbers Are Registry Positions

Chapter, section, and clause numbers are assigned positions within the legal database.

They do not normally encode:

- the date of passage;
- the ancestry affected;
- the severity of the offense;
- the enforcing institution;
- the penalty;
- the author of the law.

Those details belong in the law’s title, metadata, and statutory text.

## Sparse Numbering Is Normal

The code was assembled from:

- early Wander technical standards;
- emergency regulations;
- intersystem treaties;
- Erbium operating protocols;
- local laws adopted into the shared code;
- later amendments;
- repealed provisions whose numbers were never reused.

It is normal for a chapter to contain Sections 1, 4, 8, and 22 without containing every number between them.

It is also normal for an old technical provision to retain a very high clause number.

## Numbers Are Permanent

Once a citation has been used in the setting, its meaning should remain stable.

If the law changes:

- amend the statutory text;
- record the amendment stamp;
- preserve the original citation;
- add new clauses if the legal effect expands significantly.

Do not renumber established provisions merely to make the register look cleaner.

## Symbolic Numbers Require an In-World Reason

A number can have historical meaning, but that meaning should be recorded.

Example:

`A4%9&1-1440`

Clause 1440 may have originated as a payroll restriction covering 1,440 standard minutes, equivalent to one standard twenty-four-hour period. Later amendments expanded the provision while preserving its original registry number.

Do not choose symbolic numbers only because they look interesting.

# Administrative Title Guidance

## `A0` — General Provisions

Use for rules that define how the rest of the code is interpreted.

Common subjects:

- legal definitions;
- applicability of the code;
- recognized authorities;
- conflict between local and intersystem law;
- standards of evidence;
- liability terminology;
- definitions of person, employee, settlement, or infrastructure.

## `A1` — Standards and Records

Use when the law primarily governs information, technical compatibility, documentation, authentication, or shared standards.

Common subjects:

- [[Post-Erbium Standard]];
- stamp authority;
- language standards;
- biometric authentication;
- public records;
- relay records;
- identification formats;
- communication protocols;
- technical interoperability.

## `A2` — Personhood and Embodiment

Use when the law primarily governs who or what is legally recognized, how a person’s body is classified, or how identity persists through bodily change.

Common subjects:

- recognition among the Elect;
- legal personhood;
- ancestry classification;
- android awakening and assembly;
- astrazoan continuity;
- biological registration;
- cybernetic integration;
- cloning;
- bodily autonomy;
- medical consent;
- artificial intelligence recognition;
- sapience hearings.

## `A3` — Property and Finance

Use when the law primarily governs ownership, contracts, debts, leases, financial obligations, or asset recovery.

Common subjects:

- equipment leasing;
- ownership of body components;
- repossession;
- inherited debt;
- employee-financed equipment;
- intellectual property;
- biological patents;
- corporate claims;
- insurance;
- unpaid balances;
- ownership after death.

## `A4` — Labor and Civic Service

Use when the law primarily governs employment, compensation, workplace obligations, benefits, or compulsory service.

Common subjects:

- wages;
- service credits;
- employment classification;
- workplace safety;
- Great Volunteering;
- civic service;
- overtime;
- hazard compensation;
- shift assignments;
- corporate housing attached to employment;
- eligibility for hardship grants.

## `A5` — Travel and Communications

Use when the law primarily governs movement between systems or access to transportation and relay infrastructure.

Common subjects:

- Drift-engine licensing;
- vessel registration;
- shipping manifests;
- relay priority;
- navigation records;
- docking authority;
- transmission restrictions;
- passenger eligibility;
- emergency communications;
- off-stamp vessels.

## `A6` — Enforcement and Closure

Use when the law primarily governs investigation, detention, punishment, corporate security powers, or closure.

Common subjects:

- identity verification;
- search authority;
- detention;
- evidence seizure;
- corporate warrants;
- access denial;
- fines;
- closure;
- security jurisdiction;
- resource-security actions.

A law creating an obligation usually belongs under another title. `A6` should contain the authority to enforce it.

## `A7` — Resources and Settlements

Use when the law primarily governs land, planetary resources, extraction, environmental risk, or settlement recognition.

Common subjects:

- mineral claims;
- extraction licenses;
- survey ownership;
- environmental classifications;
- inhabited-world determinations;
- remediation;
- abandonment;
- settlement charters;
- territorial integration;
- resource security.

## `A8` — Government and Citizenship

Use when the law primarily governs civic standing, political authority, or relationships between governments.

Common subjects:

- citizenship;
- political representation;
- local government authority;
- intersystem treaties;
- residency;
- recognition of governments;
- jurisdictional disputes;
- voting eligibility;
- official civic status.

## `A9` — Emergency Authority

Use when the law activates only during declared emergencies or allows ordinary rules to be suspended.

Common subjects:

- disaster declarations;
- evacuation priority;
- continuity personnel;
- emergency requisition;
- infrastructure preservation;
- quarantine;
- emergency labor;
- communication seizure;
- suspension of local authority.

# Existing Code Register

These citations are already established and should not be reassigned.

## Common Language Standardization

**Citation:** Settled Systems Code `A1%5&99-101`

**Formal position:**

- Administrative Title 1: Standards and Records
- Chapter 5: Communication and Language Standards
- Section 99: Universal Language Specifications
- Clause 101: Adoption of Wander Slant as a foundation of Common

**Established effect:**

The pre-PES contact language known as **Accommodated-Universal Wander Slant**, commonly shortened to **Wander Slant**, was adopted as one of the foundations of Common.

**Numbering rationale:**

Section 99 and Clause 101 were inherited from a pre-PES technical standards register. Their numbers were retained when the standard was incorporated into Settled Systems law.

## Continuity Node Integrity

**Citation:** Settled Systems Code `A2%1&4-13`

**Formal position:**

- Administrative Title 2: Personhood and Embodiment
- Chapter 1: Identity and Biological Continuity
- Section 4: Continuity Identification Nodes
- Clause 13: Interference, Masking, Removal, or Falsification

**Established effect:**

Masking, removing, damaging, falsifying, or otherwise interfering with an authorized continuity identification node is prohibited.

The provision is most visibly applied to astrazoans, though its wording may apply to any population legally required to carry a continuity identifier.

## Android Assembly and Manufacture

**Citation:** Settled Systems Code `A2%13&1-3`

**Formal position:**

- Administrative Title 2: Personhood and Embodiment
- Chapter 13: Synthetic Persons and Manufactured Sapience
- Section 1: Android Assembly and Reproduction
- Clause 3: Exclusive Android Assembly Authority

**Established effect:**

Android communities possess the exclusive legal right to assemble new androids. Non-android interests are prohibited from independently manufacturing androids.

**Public purpose:**

The provision is presented as protection against the renewed manufacture of synthetic servants.

**Practical effect:**

Android communities remain dependent on Erbium-controlled foundries, components, firmware, and industrial licensing.

## Great Volunteering Compensation Restriction

**Citation:** Settled Systems Code `A4%9&1-1440`

**Formal position:**

- Administrative Title 4: Labor and Civic Service
- Chapter 9: Civic Service and Great Volunteering
- Section 1: Compensation Eligibility
- Clause 1440: Wage Ineligibility During Designated Civic Service

**Established effect:**

Personnel placed in designated civic service are ineligible for ordinary wages during the period of service.

**Numbering rationale:**

Clause 1440 originated as a payroll provision governing one standard twenty-four-hour period, equal to 1,440 standard minutes. Later amendments expanded the duration and application of the rule while retaining the original clause number.

# How to Assign a New Code

## Step 1: Identify the Actual Legal Effect

Ask what the law actually does.

Examples:

- recognizes a person;
- transfers ownership;
- requires labor;
- restricts travel;
- authorizes detention;
- grants an emergency power;
- regulates extraction;
- establishes a record standard.

Do not begin by choosing a random number.

## Step 2: Select the Controlling Administrative Title

Choose the title governing the law’s primary effect.

Examples:

A law allowing Erbium to repossess leased android components belongs under `A3`, because its primary effect concerns property and leasing.

A separate provision authorizing security personnel to enter a residence and perform that repossession belongs under `A6`, because its primary effect concerns enforcement.

## Step 3: Reuse or Create a Chapter

Check the Chapter Register.

Reuse an existing chapter whenever possible.

Create a new chapter only when the subject is distinct enough to justify a permanent legal category.

When creating a new chapter:

- choose an unused number;
- leave gaps between chapter numbers when possible;
- add the assignment to the Chapter Register;
- give the chapter a broad enough title to support future related laws.

## Step 4: Choose a Section

A section should describe the operational topic within the chapter.

Example:

Chapter `A3%4` governs Equipment and Component Leasing.

Possible sections include:

- `&1` — General Lease Formation
- `&3` — Maintenance Obligations
- `&7` — Repossession Procedures
- `&11` — Biologically Integrated Components

Sections can be inserted later without renumbering existing ones.

## Step 5: Choose a Clause

The clause contains the precise enforceable rule.

Example:

`A3%4&7-2`

- Title 3: Property and Finance
- Chapter 4: Equipment and Component Leasing
- Section 7: Repossession Procedures
- Clause 2: Repossession of Biologically or Mechanically Integrated Property

## Step 6: Record the Assignment

Every new code should be added to:

- the appropriate Chapter Register, if a new chapter was created;
- the Existing Code Register, if the provision becomes established setting canon;
- its own Obsidian law note.

## Step 7: Add Cross-References

Most significant laws interact with other titles.

Example:

A repossession law might cross-reference:

- `A2` for bodily autonomy;
- `A3` for ownership;
- `A4` for employee obligations;
- `A6` for enforcement authority;
- `A9` for emergency exceptions.

# How Settled Systems Laws Should Be Written

## Public Purpose

The public purpose explains why the law is supposedly necessary.

It should usually invoke one or more of the following:

- public safety;
- continuity of essential services;
- identity integrity;
- protection of the Elect;
- prevention of fraud;
- responsible resource stewardship;
- infrastructure stability;
- protection against another Wander-scale disaster;
- fair access;
- standardization;
- intersystem unity.

The public purpose may be sincere, manipulative, or both.

## Statutory Language

The statutory text should be:

- formal;
- emotionally neutral;
- precise in procedure;
- broad enough to support administrative discretion;
- written without openly cruel language;
- framed as a technical solution.

Prefer phrases such as:

- authorized personnel;
- designated service;
- recognized identity;
- infrastructure continuity;
- approved systems;
- reasonable verification;
- operational necessity;
- eligible parties;
- regulated access;
- compliant equipment;
- responsible stewardship;
- temporary restriction;
- extraordinary circumstances.

Avoid obvious villain language such as:

- slaves;
- disposable workers;
- punishment for dissent;
- property without rights;
- execution for inconvenience.

The law should conceal cruelty through classification and procedure.

## Administrative Interpretation

Administrative interpretation explains how Erbium regulators, courts, corporate security, or local governments apply the provision.

This may be broader than the statutory text.

Questions to answer:

- Who interprets ambiguous terms?
- What evidence is accepted?
- Which records are authoritative?
- Can Erbium issue binding guidance?
- Can local governments adopt stricter versions?
- Who receives exemptions?
- Is enforcement automatic or discretionary?

## Practical Effect

Practical effect explains what the law does to ordinary people.

Examples:

- makes employment necessary to retain housing;
- allows a company to reclaim part of a person’s body;
- turns an equipment repair into contract violation;
- prevents workers from being paid during compulsory service;
- treats unstamped labor as though it never occurred;
- forces a shapeshifter to remain identifiable to corporate systems;
- recognizes a right while making its exercise materially impossible.

## Exemptions

Erbium-controlled laws frequently include exemptions for:

- executive personnel;
- corporate security;
- continuity personnel;
- authorized research;
- emergency operations;
- licensed contractors;
- approved religious authorities;
- designated infrastructure;
- corporate medical facilities.

Exemptions should reveal who the law was designed to control.

## Penalties

Possible penalties include:

- fines;
- service-credit reduction;
- access suspension;
- employment ineligibility;
- lease acceleration;
- asset recovery;
- identity review;
- travel restriction;
- relay disconnection;
- detention;
- compulsory evaluation;
- civic service;
- closure.

Penalties may be distributed across multiple codes.

The original law establishes the violation. An `A6` provision may authorize enforcement.

# Public Law Versus GM Truth

A Settled Systems law can operate on several levels.

## Public Explanation

What citizens, employees, and players are told.

## Statutory Text

What the law technically says.

## Administrative Interpretation

How officials apply the law.

## Practical Effect

What happens in ordinary cases.

## GM Information

What the institution is hiding.

Possible GM information includes:

- the law was written for a specific corporate client;
- enforcement data is falsified;
- exemptions are sold privately;
- the law’s stated emergency no longer exists;
- a temporary provision was deliberately made permanent;
- Erbium caused the problem the law claims to solve;
- local governments lack the infrastructure needed to resist;
- an older version granted broader rights;
- the public text omits a classified implementation protocol.

Not every law needs a hidden conspiracy. Systemic incentives, ordinary cowardice, administrative convenience, and dependency should create harm without direct executive orders.
