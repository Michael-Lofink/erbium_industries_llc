You live a life of freedom, taking by force and intimidation all that you could possibly desire. You may be a privateer, raiding and plundering on behalf of a particular nation or another authority, or you may answer to no one but your own capricious whims. Either way, you rely on intimidation, wits, and combat prowess to take what you want from those you deem less deserving.

 Pirates have much in common with swashbucklers, and many pirates have the swashbuckler class or archetype. Much like a swashbuckler's daring deeds, classic pirate maneuvers are often showy feats intended to impress, intimidate, and gain panache. For example, if you have the panache class feature, Boarding Assault gains the bravado trait.

As a pirate, you sail the seas in search of enemy ships to plunder and great adventures to embark on. You gain the [Additional Lore](/Feats.aspx?ID=5114) general feat for Sailing Lore or for a specific coastal city you have a connection to (such as Port Peril Lore). You ignore the effects of [difficult terrain](/Rules.aspx?ID=2366) or [uneven ground](/Rules.aspx?ID=2370) caused by unstable ground (such as the deck of a ship). Additionally, you gain the Boarding Assault action. 

 **Boarding Assault** <actions string="Two Actions" />  ([flourish](/Traits.aspx?ID=606)) Either [Stride](/Actions.aspx?ID=2305) twice or attempt an [Acrobatics](/Skills.aspx?ID=34) check (DC determined by the GM, but usually DC 20) to swing up to twice your Speed on a rope or similar object, then [Strike](/Actions.aspx?ID=2306). If you boarded or disembarked from a boat or similar vehicle during this movement, the Strike deals one additional weapon damage die.

Spend enough time in Hell Harbor, and you pick up the paranoia encouraged by Arronax Endymion. Maybe you served under him on the _Tyrannous_, escaped his executioners, or had a friend who wasn’t so lucky; whatever the reason, you have some of his wariness. Unless you’re a willing target, you gain a +2 circumstance bonus to AC and saves against creatures you consider an ally and creatures who are [undetected](/Conditions.aspx?ID=96) by you. If a creature you thought was an ally reveals itself as an enemy, you get the same bonus against them until the end of your next turn.

You make your living as a pirate, as well as a little extra by hiring out your services to Andoran, Cheliax, or Mbe’ke. You need to recognize which ships are legitimate targets and which you need to play nice with. You can use [Sailing Lore](/Skills.aspx?ID=41) to [Recall Knowledge](/Actions.aspx?ID=2367) about creatures native to the High Seas and flags, ships, and sailors in the region. If a ship is using a false flag or other disguise to hide its true allegiances, the GM will roll a secret Sailing Lore check for you to recognize it, typically against a standard DC for the level of the ship’s captain.

Many Shackles pirates use the Eye of Abendego to great effect, but none have more familiarity with the storm than the Master of the Gales, ruler of Drenchport and five-time winner of the Free Captains’ Regatta. You’ve learned his crew’s techniques for fighting in severe weather. You reduce circumstance penalties to ranged weapon attacks due to wind or precipitation by 2. You can also use weapons you’re a master in with a –4 circumstance penalty (–2 if you’re legendary) even if wind or precipitation would normally prevent it, such as the effects of [_wall of wind_](/Spells.aspx?ID=1753).

You're particularly skilled at wielding the weapons used traditionally by pirates. You gain either the [Combat Climber](/Feats.aspx?ID=5134) or [Underwater Marauder](/Feats.aspx?ID=5230) skill feat, even if you do not meet its prerequisites. You have familiarity with the following weapons: [hatchet](/Weapons.aspx?ID=382), [rapier](/Weapons.aspx?ID=391), [scimitar](/Weapons.aspx?ID=393), and [whip](/Weapons.aspx?ID=404)—for the purposes of proficiency, you treat any of these weapons as simple weapons. Your GM may add additional martial weapons to this list as appropriate for your world or region. 

At 5th level, whenever you get a critical hit with one of these weapons, you get its [critical specialization effect](WeaponGroups.aspx).

An animal has become your loyal ally, perhaps after you liberated it from another ship or found it on a remote island. You gain the [Pet](/Feats.aspx?ID=5186) general feat. Your pet gains an additional pet Shackles Options ability, which you can choose from the standard pet abilities or the following familiar abilities: [accompanist](/Familiars.aspx?ID=108), [damage avoidance](/Familiars.aspx?ID=110), [independent](/Familiars.aspx?ID=115), [partner in crime](/Familiars.aspx?ID=119), [speech](/Familiars.aspx?ID=124), and [valet](/Familiars.aspx?ID=128). 

In addition, you learn a common 1st- or 2nd-rank spell that targets your familiar, and can cast it once per day as a [primal](/SpellLists.aspx?Tradition=4) [innate spell](/Rules.aspx?ID=2232). You can instead learn [_phase familiar_](/Spells.aspx?ID=1884) and can cast it once per hour as a primal innate spell; it’s not a focus spell for you. When you cast the chosen spell, you target your pirate’s pet instead of a familiar.

As long as you’re wearing your clothing, you gain a +1 item bonus to unarmed attack rolls, [Acrobatics](/Skills.aspx?ID=34) checks, and Reflex saves and DCs to [Escape](/Actions.aspx?ID=2296), [Grab an Edge](/Actions.aspx?ID=2313), or avoid falling [prone](/Conditions.aspx?ID=88). In addition, when you successfully [Grapple](/Actions.aspx?ID=2376) a creature, you deal slashing damage equal to your fist damage dice to that creature as you abrade their skin.

You [frighten](/Conditions.aspx?ID=76)  a foe into moving where you want them, traditionally demanding they walk the plank. Attempt to [Demoralize](/Actions.aspx?ID=2395) an opponent. On a success, in addition to the normal effects, you can also force the target to [Stride](/Actions.aspx?ID=2305) up to its Speed immediately. You choose the path the target takes, but you can't force it to move into an obviously harmful space (such as into hazardous terrain or a space where it would fall) unless your check was a critical success. As normal for forced movement, this movement doesn't trigger reactions. The target then becomes temporarily immune to Walk the Plank for 10 minutes.

The Salt Breakers learned to use whatever they could during the Vidric Revolution, and they quickly mastered the art of making a rope into a useful tool in combat. You take hold of a nearby rope, throw a rope from your hand, or fire a grappling weapon at a solid surface—and then swing up to twice your Speed. If you end your movement next to an enemy, attempt an [Athletics](/Skills.aspx?ID=3) check to [Grapple](/Actions.aspx?ID=35) that enemy, tying them up in your rope.
