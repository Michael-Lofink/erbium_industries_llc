The study of magic isn’t solely the province of tenured faculty who extend propositions or refutations in stately halls of ivory; neither is its practice limited to the dominions of hierophants atop ancient duskwood edifices or wish-apported towers of basalt. Despite their claims to authority, the eminent and elite do not define magic’s purview or purpose. Within quiet, overlooked places, magical inquiry flourishes in countless ways. Provincial villages and remote countrysides are the bailiwicks of so-called “hedge mages.” Away from the stifling support of prelates or provosts, a hedge mage may experiment with old methods in new ways, working with the quotidian in the quest to further hone practical and pragmatic spells into mighty tools.

Hedge mages tend to be a rugged, individualistic lot, whose practicality might seem an odd fit with the idiosyncrasies and idealism so common to their number. Without comforts of station or stipend, hedge mages must make do with what’s readily accessible or easily available, mastering unremarkable materials and processes to achieve extraordinary magical effects. With practice, everyday cantrips and catalysts become reliable and versatile tools. Many hedge mages apply a mixture of simple spells, scholastic application, and hands-on knowledge to solve their communities’ problems, be it blighted crops and plagued cattle or hauntings and monster attacks. Those few who become adventurer find their self-sufficient and resourceful lifestyles to be ample training for that career’s challenges and uncertainties, bringing their unorthodox, yet undeniably effective, blend of magic and common sense to face the dangers of battlefield and dungeon.

Most hedge mages come from the ranks of classes that don’t inherently grant spellcasting. Many of them begin their adventuring careers as [fighters](/Classes.aspx?ID=35), [rangers](/Classes.aspx?ID=36), and [rogues](/Classes.aspx?ID=37), though a few [investigators](/Classes.aspx?ID=59) and [swashbucklers](/Classes.aspx?ID=63) round out the mix. Occasionally, a rural [alchemist](/Classes.aspx?ID=56) will add hedge mage spellcasting to their repertoire. Hedge mages who delve deep into the practice of momentary magic might also become [scroll tricksters](/Archetypes.aspx?ID=262) or [talisman dabblers](/Archetypes.aspx?ID=266).

[The Universe](/Planes.aspx?ID=26) is your laboratory and altar, and the world your workshop and study. Grounding your theoretical studies in honest labor, you work with the mundane to draw forth their hidden magic. When you take this dedication, choose [Arcana](/Skills.aspx?ID=35), [Nature](/Skills.aspx?ID=43), [Occultism](/Skills.aspx?ID=44), or [Religion](/Skills.aspx?ID=46). You become trained in that skill, or you become expert if you were already trained. You also gain a keepsake that helps your magical studies; this keepsake is something that has perhaps been handed down to you but to which you’ve contributed a few personal touches, though it can be anything appropriate to your personal magical style. Sample keepsakes include a folio or binder of magical observations, a walking stick with mnemonic grooves, a bracelet of assorted charms and baubles, or a planchette carved into shape of a coffin. If it’s destroyed or lost, you can create a new keepsake with 1 day of downtime.

You gain the [Cast a Spell](/Actions.aspx?ID=3521) activity. You’re trained in the spell attack modifier and spell DC statistics. Your key spellcasting attribute for hedge mage archetype spells is your choice of Intelligence or Wisdom, and they are hedge mage spells of a tradition associated with the skill chosen for this dedication ([arcane](/SpellLists.aspx?Tradition=1) for Arcana, [primal](/SpellLists.aspx?Tradition=4) for Nature, [occult](/SpellLists.aspx?Tradition=3) for Occultism, and [divine](/SpellLists.aspx?Tradition=2) for Religion).

Your keepsake holds 4 common cantrips of your choice from your appropriate tradition. You prepare spells each day by meditating with your keepsake. You can prepare two cantrips from your keepsake each day. You can only add cantrips to your keepsake with the [Learn a Spell](/Actions.aspx?ID=2366) activity or by other similar means.

Want not, waste not; through a mix of frugality, inspiration, and sheer resourcefulness, you maintain a catalog of materials with ambient, lingering magic, which you can transform into temporary [spell catalysts](/Rules.aspx?ID=1509). Be it powder ground from the burnt remains of hippogriff feathers or yarn drawn from the locks of an [alicorn](/Monsters.aspx?ID=3320), you have these materials at hand and, perhaps more importantly, know how to bend them toward practical effects. These [consumable](/Traits.aspx?ID=564) items can change or enhance the effects of specific spells. To get the most out of a spell catalyst, you or an ally should be able to cast the affected spell.

You know the formulas for all common spell catalysts of your level or lower. You remember spell catalyst formulas and don’t need a formula book for them. Each day during your daily preparations, you can make two spell catalysts with an item level no higher than half your level. You must know each spell catalyst’s formula. A spell catalyst created this way is a temporary item and loses its magic the next time you make your [daily preparations](/Rules.aspx?ID=2575) if you haven’t already used it.
