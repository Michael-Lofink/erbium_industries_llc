# The Fall of Wander and the Rise of Erbium Industries

> [!summary]
> **The Star Dream** revealed the means of intersystem travel to the peoples who became known as **the Elect**. Wander possessed the erbium required to turn that revelation into functioning technology. The extraction of that resource destroyed Wander and allowed [[The Fall of Wander and the Rise of Erbium Industries]] to become the dominant corporate, political, and technological power of the [[Settled Systems]].

# Public Record

> [!info] Player-Facing History
> The following account is taught throughout the Settled Systems, distributed through Erbium-operated education networks, and repeated in corporate recruitment material.

## The Revelation

Before the Star Dream, the peoples of the galaxy looked into the night without knowing how to cross the distances between them.

Then came **the Star Dream**.

Across many worlds, individuals awoke carrying the same revelation: visions of distant stars, unfamiliar peoples, and a hyperspatial plane through which those distances could be crossed. Within the Dream were the principles required to construct Drift engines, intersystem relay networks, advanced navigation systems, and other technologies that would unite the chosen peoples of the galaxy.

Those who received the revelation became known collectively as **the Elect**.

The Elect did not share a single ancestry, government, or religion. What united them was the belief that the Star Dream had called them to find one another and establish a civilization among the stars.

The Dream gave them purpose and erbium would give them passage.

## Wander and Element 68

Every known Drift engine and intersystem relay required element 68: **erbium**.

Wander possessed the largest and most accessible erbium deposits ever discovered. Unusual magmatic processes had concentrated the element into vast formations throughout the planet’s crust, placing Wander at the center of the emerging intersystem age.

The peoples of Wander became the first great suppliers of Drift-compatible erbium. Their mines, foundries, research facilities, and launch complexes provided the material through which the revelation of the Star Dream could be made real.

Ships left Wander carrying refined erbium to distant worlds. Those worlds built engines of their own, entered the Drift, and joined the growing community of the Elect.

Wander became the material heart of the Settled Systems.

## The Founding of Erbium Industries

As demand increased, Wander’s separate mining, refining, transportation, and engineering operations struggled to coordinate production.

Erbium Industries LLC was formed to organize those efforts.

The company united extraction firms, Drift engineers, refinery operators, relay technicians, and intersystem transportation contractors under a shared administrative structure. Erbium Industries established standards for refinement, improved distribution, and ensured that even distant members of the Elect could receive the materials needed to answer the Star Dream.

The company’s early leadership described erbium as more than a commodity. It was a sacred civic responsibility entrusted to Wander for the benefit of the Settled Systems.

Erbium Industries adopted that responsibility as its corporate mandate.

> [!quote] Early Erbium Industries Charter
> No world received the Star Dream so that it might remain alone. Wander has been entrusted with the means by which the Elect may reach one another, and Erbium Industries shall ensure that this trust is honored.

## The Wander Geological Disaster

The full structure of Wander’s crust was poorly understood during the early Drift era.

As extraction operations expanded, mining teams encountered increasingly unstable formations beneath the surface. Geological failures intensified across several major production regions, culminating in a cascading series of earthquakes, collapses, volcanic events, and atmospheric contamination.

The disaster devastated Wander.

Population centers were destroyed, transportation networks failed, and large portions of the planet became uninhabitable. Millions were displaced, and much of Wander’s cultural and historical record was lost.

Official investigations concluded that the catastrophe resulted from geological processes that contemporary science had been unable to predict. Early Drift civilization had demanded resources faster than its understanding of planetary engineering could develop.

Wander became the first great sacrifice of the intersystem age.

## Erbium’s Response

Erbium Industries suffered extensive losses during the collapse. Mines, refineries, offices, equipment, and personnel were destroyed alongside the cities and communities they served.

The company’s surviving vessels and relay stations became essential to the emergency response.

Erbium Industries coordinated evacuations, transported refugees, restored communications, and distributed refined reserves to systems that would otherwise have lost access to Drift travel. Its relay network allowed governments throughout the Settled Systems to communicate during the crisis.

Without Erbium Industries, the young intersystem civilization created by the Elect might have collapsed with Wander.

## Corporate Stewardship

Following the disaster, the governments of the Settled Systems granted Erbium Industries expanded authority over the remaining supply of element 68.

The company was given responsibility for:

- locating new erbium deposits;
- maintaining strategic reserves;
- licensing Drift-compatible refinement;
- distributing fuel and components;
- operating intersystem relay networks;
- preserving the technological teachings of the Star Dream;
- preventing another planetary extraction disaster.

These powers were originally described as emergency measures. As the Settled Systems became increasingly dependent on Erbium infrastructure, they developed into permanent institutions.

Erbium Industries now describes itself as the steward of the material legacy of the Star Dream.

Its control of erbium is presented as a necessary protection against scarcity, conflict, unsafe extraction, and the collapse of intersystem civilization.

> [!quote] Erbium Industries Public Archive
> **The Star Dream showed us the way. Wander gave us the means. Erbium Industries carries that responsibility forward.**
>
> **Bring home the stars.**

## The Elect and the Settled Systems

The Star Dream became the ideological foundation of the Settled Systems.

Governments, corporations, and religious authorities increasingly treated reception of the Dream as evidence that a people possessed a place within intersystem civilization. The Elect were understood to have been chosen, invited, or recognized by whatever force had created the revelation.

Those who had not experienced the Star Dream were often considered spiritually incomplete, technologically unprepared, or unfit for full participation in the Settled Systems.

This doctrine shaped laws governing:

- legal personhood;
- citizenship;
- migration;
- intersystem travel;
- access to Drift infrastructure;
- political representation;
- eligibility for corporate service;
- recognition as a sapient people.

During Lapse 25 PES, androids throughout the Settled Systems experienced the Star Dream simultaneously. Their awakening forced governments to recognize that androids possessed independent thought and belonged among the Elect.

Androids were subsequently granted conditional legal personhood.

The event is commonly celebrated as proof that the Star Dream can reveal new members of the Elect when they are ready to join the Settled Systems.

# GM Information

> [!danger] GM Only
> The public history of Wander is a corporate narrative assembled after the disaster. Erbium Industries did not merely survive the destruction of Wander. Its leadership knowingly contributed to the collapse, protected its own assets before the public was warned, and then used the catastrophe to secure permanent control over intersystem civilization.

## The Star Dream as Political Authority

The Star Dream was a genuine shared revelation, but its religious meaning was never as unified as the public record suggests.

Different peoples interpreted it as:

- a divine message;
- contact with an unknown intelligence;
- a psychic contagion;
- a transmitted memory;
- evidence of a shared cosmic consciousness;
- a technological instruction package whose source remained unknown.

Erbium Industries encouraged the interpretation most useful to its interests: the Star Dream had chosen the Elect, the Elect were destined to build the Settled Systems, and erbium was the material through which that destiny could be fulfilled.

This placed Erbium Industries in a uniquely powerful position.

The company did not claim to have created the revelation. It claimed to be the institution capable of carrying it out.

By controlling erbium, Drift components, and relay infrastructure, Erbium placed itself between the Elect and the future they believed had been promised to them.

Over time, corporate propaganda blurred the distinction between obedience to the Star Dream and dependence on Erbium Industries.

Resistance to the company could therefore be portrayed as:

- opposition to intersystem unity;
- rejection of Wander’s sacrifice;
- obstruction of the Elect’s destiny;
- a threat to Drift access;
- interference with the sacred infrastructure of civilization.

## The Creation of the Monopoly

Erbium Industries began as a consortium of Wander-based mining companies, refineries, transportation firms, technical laboratories, and relay contractors.

Its formation was presented as a practical response to rapidly increasing demand.

In reality, the consortium’s founders understood that control of element 68 meant control over nearly every society dependent upon the Star Dream’s technologies.

Erbium Industries consolidated power through:

- purchasing smaller extraction companies;
- forcing independent mines into exclusive processing contracts;
- acquiring patents related to Drift-compatible refinement;
- controlling off-world transportation;
- buying or absorbing relay operators;
- lobbying Wander’s governments against production restrictions;
- requiring new systems to purchase Erbium-certified components;
- withholding refined material from competitors;
- tying infrastructure access to long-term corporate agreements.

By the time Wander collapsed, Erbium Industries controlled most legal extraction on the planet and nearly all large-scale refinement.

The monopoly already existed. The catastrophe made it permanent.

## What Erbium Knew

Wander’s erbium deposits were not isolated veins that could be removed without consequence.

The element had accumulated within deep magmatic formations that distributed pressure across several major regions of the planet’s crust. As those formations were hollowed out, stress shifted into neighboring fault systems.

Erbium geologists documented:

- accelerating subsidence around extraction zones;
- abnormal fault movement;
- changes in volcanic pressure;
- instability beneath major population centers;
- structural collapse in exhausted mining regions;
- atmospheric contamination from deep-crust breaches.

Early internal reports warned of regional disasters.

Later reports predicted the possibility of cascading crustal failure and widespread planetary devastation.

Erbium’s leadership did not know the exact stamp at which Wander would collapse. They knew that continued extraction created an unacceptable probability of catastrophe.

They continued anyway.

## The Suppression Campaign

Erbium Industries classified the geological findings as proprietary operational data.

The company then:

- transferred dissenting geologists away from active projects;
- terminated researchers who refused to revise their conclusions;
- funded alternative studies that minimized the risk;
- restricted independent access to extraction zones;
- pressured public agencies to delay inspections;
- blamed early collapses on local operators and natural seismic activity;
- increased extraction quotas before new regulations could take effect;
- removed geological records from public archives;
- threatened settlements that discussed halting production.

Corporate leadership argued internally that reducing extraction would destabilize the Settled Systems, weaken Wander’s political position, and allow rival interests to seize control of the erbium supply.

Some executives also believed the planet could survive several more generations of mining.

Others did not care whether it could.

## Selective Evacuation

Before the public received any warning, Erbium Industries began quietly relocating assets from Wander.

The company transferred:

- refined erbium reserves;
- proprietary refining equipment;
- Drift-engine prototypes;
- relay components;
- executive personnel;
- selected engineers and technical specialists;
- financial archives;
- legal records establishing off-world ownership claims.

These transfers were recorded as routine expansion, emergency redundancy, or infrastructure development.

Families of executives and essential personnel were given passage off-world. Most mining communities received no warning.

When civilian evacuation orders were finally issued, the company’s most valuable assets were already gone.

## The Collapse

The final disaster began when Erbium Industries expanded extraction into several interconnected deep-crust formations that its own geologists had identified as critical.

The removal of those formations redistributed pressure across already unstable faults. Sections of the crust collapsed into exhausted mining regions, triggering seismic failures and breaching active magmatic zones.

The devastation spread faster than public agencies could respond.

Cities collapsed. Atmosphere-processing networks failed. Volcanic emissions contaminated populated regions. Ports and launch sites were destroyed or overwhelmed.

Erbium rescue operations saved many lives, but corporate evacuation priorities favored personnel, equipment, and reserves considered essential to continued operations.

The company became both a principal cause of the catastrophe and the institution best positioned to manage its aftermath.

## Control of the Record

Erbium Industries controlled the relay network through which news of Wander’s destruction reached other systems.

The company determined:

- which reports were transmitted;
- which experts appeared before public inquiries;
- which data sets were recognized as authentic;
- which timestamps were accepted as official;
- which evacuation records remained accessible;
- which geological models were preserved;
- which witnesses were dismissed as unreliable.

Government investigations depended upon Erbium servers, Erbium technical staff, and Erbium-funded experts.

The final reports described the collapse as an unforeseeable interaction between primitive extraction methods and poorly understood planetary geology.

No individual executive was found criminally responsible.

## Manufacturing Permanent Scarcity

Wander’s destruction created a genuine erbium shortage.

Erbium Industries ensured that the shortage could never be resolved outside its control.

The company used its surviving reserves and infrastructure to:

- control the rate at which stockpiles entered the market;
- purchase bankrupt competitors;
- acquire claims to newly discovered deposits;
- suppress independent geological surveys;
- require new mines to use Erbium refinement facilities;
- sabotage or discredit alternative technologies;
- classify extraction data as corporate property;
- tie relay access to exclusive supply agreements;
- lobby for laws prohibiting unlicensed Drift-component production.

Erbium justified each measure as necessary to prevent unsafe extraction and another disaster like Wander.

The company that destroyed a planet through overextraction became the authority responsible for deciding where extraction was safe.

## The Corporate Religion

Erbium Industries gradually positioned itself as the practical custodian of the Star Dream.

The company does not officially identify as a religious institution. Its propaganda borrows heavily from religious language:

- revelation;
- stewardship;
- sacrifice;
- unity;
- purpose;
- the Elect;
- the shared future;
- the sacred responsibility of expansion.

Corporate ceremonies commemorate Wander’s destruction as a sacrifice that made the Settled Systems possible.

Employees are taught that their labor continues Wander’s mission. Miners, relay technicians, and Drift engineers are described as participants in a destiny revealed by the Star Dream.

Erbium’s monopoly is therefore defended through both economic necessity and spiritual legitimacy.

To question the company’s authority is to question whether Wander’s sacrifice had meaning.

## Personhood and the Elect

The doctrine of the Elect became one of the most effective tools of oppression in the Settled Systems.

Rather than recognizing personhood through consciousness, autonomy, or the capacity to suffer, many governments required evidence that a people had participated in the Star Dream.

This allowed governments and corporations to deny rights to groups they found inconvenient.

Those excluded from the Elect could be classified as:

- nonsapient;
- insufficiently developed;
- spiritually unrecognized;
- dangerous imitations of personhood;
- property;
- wildlife;
- unauthorized artificial intelligence.

Androids were treated as manufactured servants until Lapse 25 PES, when they experienced the Star Dream.

Their awakening made continued denial of their personhood politically dangerous. If the Star Dream had reached androids, then denying their place among the Elect would undermine the religious authority of the revelation itself.

Androids were granted conditional rights because the Star Dream validated them.

The law did not establish that all sentient beings deserved personhood.

It established that beings recognized by the revelation could earn a place within the Settled Systems.

This distinction remains central to the oppression of peoples who have never experienced the Dream.

## Surviving Evidence

Evidence of Erbium’s role in Wander’s destruction still exists.

Possible records include:

- geological projections predicting cascading crustal failure;
- extraction quotas approved after those projections were received;
- correspondence ordering researchers to alter their findings;
- transport manifests showing reserves moved off-world before public warnings;
- executive evacuation authorizations;
- relay logs with deleted or delayed civilian distress transmissions;
- internal discussions about acceptable population loss;
- contracts claiming ownership of off-world deposits before the collapse;
- testimony from miners, engineers, or officials who survived;
- unrevised geological data stored in abandoned Wander facilities;
- pre-collapse religious writings rejecting Erbium’s interpretation of the Star Dream.

Erbium classifies possession or distribution of these materials as corporate espionage, infrastructure sabotage, theft of proprietary data, or dissemination of destabilizing misinformation.

## The Meaning of the Slogan

**“Bring home the stars”** began as a promise that the peoples of Wander would explore the galaxy and return with knowledge, resources, and new relationships.

Erbium Industries kept the slogan after Wander was destroyed.

In public, it honors the world that made intersystem travel possible.

In truth, Erbium Industries destroyed the home to which the stars were supposed to be brought.
