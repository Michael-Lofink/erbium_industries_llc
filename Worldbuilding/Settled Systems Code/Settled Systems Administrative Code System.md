# Settled Systems Administrative Code

The **Settled Systems Code** is the shared law used across the [[Settled Systems]]. It governs rights and obligations between people, institutions, and governments. Erbium administers much of the infrastructure used to apply it; that does not give the company sole power to make law.

Start with the [[Code Index]] to find a provision. Each page explains its effect and links to the notes that use it. [[Code Inbox]] lists new citations and unfinished provisions.

## Reading a citation

`A2%13&1-3` means **Title 2, Chapter 13, Section 1, Clause 3**: the rule giving android communities exclusive assembly rights.

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `A2` | Administrative Title: the broad area of law. |
| `%13` | Chapter: a subject within that title. |
| `&1` | Section: a narrower topic. |
| `-3` | Clause: the particular rule. |

The title number tells you the field of law. The remaining numbers are permanent registry positions. They do not encode the penalty, the affected ancestry, or the date. Gaps are normal because the Code incorporates older standards, treaties, and amendments.

A letter after a citation identifies a subclause. For example, `A2%1&4-13.g` covers emergency medical interference with a continuity node. It belongs to the parent provision.

Separate different citations with commas. Two periods indicate an inclusive clause range: `A2%1&4-13..16` means Clauses 13 through 16. This is a formatting example; it does not establish the contents of Clauses 14–16. <!-- ssac:ignore -->

## Administrative titles

| Title | Field |
| --- | --- |
| `A0` | Definitions, interpretation, and general jurisdiction |
| `A1` | Standards, records, language, timekeeping, and authentication |
| `A2` | Personhood, ancestry, identity, biology, and medical status |
| `A3` | Property, finance, leasing, debt, and contracts |
| `A4` | Employment, wages, civic service, benefits, and workplace obligations |
| `A5` | Travel, transport, navigation, communications, and relays |
| `A6` | Investigation, detention, enforcement, penalties, and closure |
| `A7` | Land, settlements, extraction, resources, and environmental regulation |
| `A8` | Government, citizenship, political representation, and intersystem agreements |
| `A9` | Emergency powers, evacuation, requisition, and quarantine |

See [[Code Chapters]] for existing chapter assignments. A rule creating an obligation normally belongs under its subject title. A separate enforcement power belongs under A6.

[[Code Index]] replaces the old partial register. Citation numbers already used in the notes are reserved, including provisional references and worked examples. Their status is shown separately:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Established | The source notes describe it as law. The page may summarize those notes rather than supply full statutory text. |
| Provisional | A source reserves the citation and subject, but the law still needs development. |
| Example | A drafting exercise, not established law. |
| Unreviewed | Automatically detected or newly entered; its meaning has not been checked. |

## Adding or changing a law

Write what the rule requires, permits, or prohibits in one sentence. Check the index for an existing provision, then use [[Quick Code Entry]] if a new one is needed. Choose the title by legal effect and reuse a suitable chapter from [[Code Chapters]]. Assign an unused section and clause without renumbering existing laws.

Keep the explanation short. Record who applies the rule, its relevant limits, and what happens in practice when those details are established. Unknown penalties, exemptions, dates, and authorities can remain unspecified. Official wording is optional; label newly drafted statutory text as proposed.

Amendments keep the original citation. Record the change and its stamp when known. Add another clause if the new rule has a substantially different effect.

A provision can address a real problem and still be applied unfairly. Describe the actual mechanism of that unfairness. Avoid adding a conspiracy or an exhaustive list of possible abuses to every law.
