# Laws in your character's backstory

You can describe a law by what it did to your character. You do not need to invent a citation or write legal language.

Give the GM three things:

1. **The rule:** what was required, forbidden, or allowed.
2. **The effect:** what happened to your character because of it.
3. **The unresolved issue:** whether there is still a debt, restriction, dispute, or obligation.

For example: “When my job ended, I lost my housing sponsor. My family stayed with relatives while I challenged the record.” That is enough to start. The GM can connect it to an existing law and settle any missing details.

## Finding an existing law

Search [[Code Index]] by its ordinary name. Open the provision for a short explanation and its source notes. An **established** entry is already described as law; **provisional**, **example**, and **unreviewed** entries still need GM confirmation.

A citation such as `A2%13&1-3` identifies Title 2, Chapter 13, Section 1, Clause 3. You do not need to memorize the format. When the exact rule is unknown, “Title Four labor law” or the chapter stem `A4%5` is enough to identify the subject. A chapter reference does not establish the details of a new rule.

## Proposing something new

Use the description above and leave the number open. The GM will check for conflicts and choose an unused citation. Avoid inventing penalties, deadlines, or blanket exemptions unless those details matter to the story you are proposing.

If an official's actions exceeded the law, say so. What a company claims, what the law permits, and what happened to your character may differ.
