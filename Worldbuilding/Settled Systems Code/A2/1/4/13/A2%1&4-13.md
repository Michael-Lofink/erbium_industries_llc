# Continuity Identification Node Integrity and Anti-Falsification Provision

> [!summary]
> Settled Systems Code `A2%1&4-13` prohibits the deliberate masking, suppression, removal, alteration, duplication, falsification, or unauthorized redirection of an approved continuity identification node.
>
> A failed or unreadable node permits temporary identity restrictions while the bearer’s continuity is investigated. It does not independently establish guilt.
>
> Deliberate continuity fraud involving deaths, mass-casualty risk, critical infrastructure, or the concealment of a killing may be referred for **closure eligibility review**. Closure is a sentence of death and may be imposed only through the procedures established under Administrative Title 6.

## Citation

## Plain-Language Explanation

A person carrying an authorized continuity node may not deliberately prevent it from identifying them, connect it to a false identity, copy its credentials, or produce a fraudulent continuity response.

An unreadable node does not automatically make its bearer a criminal. Authorities may, however, place the bearer under **unverified continuity status** and restrict access to sensitive systems until their identity is confirmed.

Ordinary violations can result in fines, access suspension, detention, civic service, or criminal prosecution. A case becomes eligible for closure only when prosecutors establish the additional aggravating circumstances required by this clause and Administrative Title 6.

> [!quote] Settled Systems Continuity Registry
> Your appearance may change. Your occupation may change. Your body may change.
>
> Continuity verification ensures that your rights, records, and responsibilities remain yours.

# Public Purpose

The Settled Systems publicly justify continuity nodes as a response to the limitations of ordinary biometric authentication.

Visual recognition, fingerprints, retinal scans, voiceprints, genetic sampling, skeletal measurements, implanted credentials, and behavioral analysis become unreliable when a person can:

- alter their external appearance;
- redistribute or replace internal organs;
- transfer into a replacement body;
- integrate extensive cybernetics;
- assume a legally authorized remembrance;
- undergo substantial medical reconstruction;
- produce near-perfect biological mimicry.

Continuity nodes are presented as a means of protecting a person’s identity across these changes.

The official purposes of Clause 13 are to:

- prevent identity theft and impersonation;
- preserve legal continuity through bodily alteration;
- protect restricted infrastructure from unauthorized access;
- prevent misuse of remembrance skins;
- preserve the integrity of medical, financial, and employment records;
- prevent one person from inheriting another person’s property, debts, warrants, or obligations through physical resemblance;
- prevent altered bodies from being used to evade lawful accountability;
- maintain public trust in relay-connected identification systems.

Erbium Industries argues that the system permits bodily freedom by allowing variable-form people to alter their appearance without losing access to their legal identity.

Critics respond that the freedom exists only while Erbium retains the ability to identify the person beneath every body they assume.

# Statutory Text

## Clause 13: Interference, Masking, Removal, or Falsification

### `A2%1&4-13.a` — Prohibited Interference

No person shall knowingly mask, suppress, redirect, alter, duplicate, disable, remove, damage, or render materially nonresponsive an authorized continuity identification node for the purpose of:

1. concealing a recognized identity;
2. preventing lawful continuity verification;
3. misrepresenting the identity of the bearer;
4. transferring a continuity credential to another person;
5. preventing detection of an applicable legal restriction;
6. obtaining access through a false or incomplete continuity response.

Physical damage alone does not establish intent.

### `A2%1&4-13.b` — Falsified Continuity Response

No person shall knowingly manufacture, possess, install, transmit, or employ hardware, software, biological tissue, encoded credentials, relay instructions, or other means intended to produce a continuity response associated with another recognized person.

Possession alone does not establish a violation when the relevant device, data, or material has an authorized medical, research, repair, archival, security, or remembrance function.

### `A2%1&4-13.c` — Unauthorized Continuity Transfer

No continuity node, continuity key, remembrance authorization, or associated registry credential may be transferred between persons without prior authorization from the Settled Systems Continuity Registry.

A continuity credential remains associated with the registered legal person regardless of:

- changes in external appearance;
- changes in ancestry presentation;
- cybernetic modification;
- tissue replacement;
- medical reconstruction;
- occupation;
- registered service identity;
- remembrance assignment.

### `A2%1&4-13.d` — Duty to Report an Integrity Event

A person who knows or reasonably suspects that their continuity node has been damaged, altered, duplicated, accessed without authorization, or rendered unreliable must report the integrity event within 120 standard minutes of discovering it.

The bearer must submit to approved continuity validation within 1,440 standard minutes or before requesting access to controlled infrastructure, whichever occurs first.

When the bearer is off-stamp, the reporting period begins upon the first successful connection to a recognized stamp authority.

### `A2%1&4-13.e` — Unverified Continuity Status

Refusal to submit to a lawfully authorized continuity scan, or failure of a scan to produce a valid continuity response, permits an authorized official to classify the bearer as having **unverified continuity status**.

Unverified continuity status is an administrative classification. It does not independently establish criminal intent, infiltrator status, or eligibility for closure.

While the classification remains active, authorities may temporarily restrict the bearer’s access to:

- intersystem transportation;
- relay services;
- controlled worksites;
- medical records;
- financial accounts;
- corporate employment systems;
- secured residential areas;
- equipment registered to the disputed identity;
- remembrance-authorized systems.

### `A2%1&4-13.f` — Unverified Infiltrator Designation

A bearer may be designated an **unverified infiltrator** only when unverified continuity is accompanied by reasonable evidence that the bearer has deliberately attempted to:

- enter controlled infrastructure;
- exercise authority belonging to another person;
- conceal an unauthorized remembrance;
- bypass an active warrant or security restriction;
- access another person’s financial, medical, employment, or ownership records;
- transmit a falsified continuity credential;
- conceal the identity, death, disappearance, or replacement of another person.

An unreadable node, refusal to scan, or unregistered bodily change is insufficient by itself to establish infiltrator status.

### `A2%1&4-13.g` — Emergency Medical Interference

A licensed medical professional may disable, remove, shield, alter, or otherwise interfere with a continuity node when necessary to prevent:

- death;
- irreversible neurological injury;
- catastrophic tissue rejection;
- interference with emergency surgery;
- comparable and immediate bodily harm.

The professional must:

1. preserve the removed node or its recoverable components;
2. create a stamped medical incident record;
3. document the medical necessity;
4. report the intervention at the first medically reasonable opportunity;
5. assist the patient in obtaining temporary continuity certification.

A medically necessary intervention does not constitute a violation of this clause.

### `A2%1&4-13.h` — Accidental Damage

A person is not criminally liable solely because their node was damaged through:

- workplace injury;
- illness;
- equipment failure;
- environmental exposure;
- violence committed against them;
- medical complications;
- another event outside their reasonable control.

Administrative restrictions may remain in effect until continuity is reestablished.

The cost of inspection, replacement, reimplantation, and temporary certification may be assigned under applicable employment, medical, insurance, or equipment agreements.

### `A2%1&4-13.i` — Authorized Variance

The Settled Systems Continuity Registry may authorize controlled suppression, removal, substitution, or restricted output of a continuity node for:

- intelligence operations;
- witness protection;
- protected diplomatic activity;
- authorized continuity research;
- emergency medical trials;
- corporate or civil security operations;
- remembrance testing;
- infrastructure security testing;
- another activity designated as necessary to protect the Settled Systems.

A variance may be sealed from the bearer, local authorities, ordinary query systems, or public review when disclosure would allegedly defeat its authorized purpose.

### `A2%1&4-13.j` — Aggravated Continuity Interference

A violation of this clause is aggravated when committed for the purpose of:

- assuming control of life-support, power-generation, navigation, relay, medical, or defense infrastructure;
- exercising authority through an unauthorized remembrance;
- diverting controlled property, weapons, vessels, or financial assets;
- concealing the death, killing, disappearance, or unlawful replacement of another person;
- fabricating evidence that another person remains alive;
- evading lawful detention;
- evading an existing closure order;
- facilitating an organized attack against a settlement, vessel, station, or recognized government.

Aggravated continuity interference permits immediate detention and enhanced criminal prosecution under Administrative Title 6.

### `A2%1&4-13.k` — Closure Eligibility

A violation of this clause may be referred for closure eligibility review only when the prosecuting authority alleges and produces evidence that the accused knowingly committed aggravated continuity interference and that the conduct:

1. directly caused the death of another person;
2. was committed with the intent to cause one or more deaths;
3. created a substantial and imminent risk of multiple deaths through interference with critical infrastructure;
4. concealed a killing in a manner that materially prevented rescue, investigation, or protection of additional victims;
5. materially assisted another offense already designated as closure-eligible under Administrative Title 6.

Closure eligibility is not a finding of guilt and does not itself authorize execution.

### `A2%1&4-13.l` — Limitation on Closure

No person may be sentenced to closure solely for:

- possessing an altered or damaged node;
- refusing an ordinary scan;
- remaining off-stamp;
- using an unregistered appearance;
- failing to report an integrity event;
- receiving unauthorized medical treatment;
- attempting to avoid ancestry disclosure;
- committing nonviolent identity fraud;
- entering infrastructure without causing or intending fatal harm.

Closure may be imposed only after a final judgment under Settled Systems Code `A6%8&1-3` and completion or exhaustion of the review procedures required by Administrative Title 6.

# Definitions

## Authorized Continuity Node

An implanted, integrated, or otherwise body-bound identification device approved by the Settled Systems Continuity Registry.

The node is designed to remain identifiable through ordinary bodily alteration and provides a cryptographically authenticated continuity response.

A continuity node does not ordinarily store a complete identity file. It transmits an encrypted continuity key that permits an authorized system to retrieve information from the relevant registry.

## Continuity Key

The cryptographic credential assigned to an authorized node and linked to a recognized legal person.

A continuity key is intended to confirm that the present bearer remains the same registered person despite changes in appearance, anatomy, or bodily configuration.

## Continuity Response

The result returned when an approved system queries a continuity node and compares its key, integrity state, and registry status against recognized records.

Possible responses include:

- verified;
- verified with restrictions;
- remembrance-authorized;
- expired authorization;
- integrity warning;
- record unavailable;
- continuity disputed;
- unverified;
- detained identity;
- referred for closure review.

## Integrity Event

Any occurrence that may compromise the reliability of a continuity node or its associated registry record.

Examples include:

- physical damage;
- unauthorized access;
- suspected duplication;
- medical removal;
- corrupted firmware;
- relay disagreement;
- contradictory registry records;
- unexplained continuity responses;
- a node remaining active after the bearer’s recorded death.

## Masking

Any deliberate act that prevents an otherwise functional node from being detected, read, authenticated, or connected to its recognized registry record.

Administrative guidance may classify the following as masking:

- signal-shielding garments;
- unauthorized medical insulation;
- incompatible cybernetic housings;
- relay jamming;
- deliberate tissue placement around the node;
- software intended to delay authentication;
- repeated avoidance of query range;
- remaining off-stamp specifically to avoid an active verification order.

## Falsification

Any deliberate act intended to produce a continuity response that inaccurately identifies:

- who is present;
- which person a body belongs to;
- whether a remembrance is authorized;
- whether a node remains intact;
- whether a registered person is alive;
- whether a bearer is subject to restrictions, detention, or closure.

## Unverified Continuity Status

A temporary administrative classification applied when authorities cannot establish a reliable connection between a body and a recognized legal identity.

A person under unverified continuity status remains legally alive and retains legal personhood. Access to services may nevertheless be suspended until the discrepancy is resolved.

## Unverified Infiltrator

A security classification applied when an unresolved continuity discrepancy is accompanied by evidence of deliberate identity concealment, unauthorized access, or fraudulent exercise of another person’s authority.

The designation permits detention and investigation. It is not a conviction.

## Continuity Suspension

The temporary restriction of a person’s access to records, employment, transportation, financial systems, controlled infrastructure, and other relay-connected services while their identity remains disputed.

Continuity suspension is not closure.

## Controlled Infrastructure

Any location, system, vehicle, relay, facility, database, or machinery designated as requiring verified continuity.

This commonly includes:

- Drift vessels;
- ports and docking facilities;
- relay stations;
- power-generation systems;
- life-support controls;
- medical archives;
- corporate foundries;
- extraction-control systems;
- restricted research facilities;
- remembrance-authorized systems.

## Critical Infrastructure

Controlled infrastructure whose interruption, misuse, destruction, or unauthorized operation could reasonably cause:

- loss of life;
- loss of atmosphere or environmental containment;
- uncontrolled Drift transit;
- loss of navigation;
- mass communication failure;
- medical-system failure;
- reactor or power-grid failure;
- another widespread threat to survival.

## Closure Eligibility Review

A capital proceeding conducted under Administrative Title 6 to determine whether an offense legally permits a sentence of closure.

A referral does not establish guilt and does not itself impose a death sentence.

## Closure

A sentence of death imposed by a recognized Settled Systems tribunal and carried out by an authorized authority after completion or exhaustion of the required review process.

Official language describes closure as the final resolution of a threat to civic continuity and as a means by which affected communities may proceed without continued exposure to the offender.

Critics identify the term as a euphemism that frames execution as healing, resolution, or assistance to victims while obscuring the deliberate killing performed by the state.

# Administrative Interpretation

The Settled Systems Continuity Registry interprets Clause 13 broadly.

An individual does not need to physically damage a node to be investigated for interference. Any deliberate act that materially reduces the availability, reliability, or authority of a continuity response may trigger review.

Erbium Industries relay systems act as the primary technical authority in most continuity disputes. Local governments may operate their own readers, but those readers usually validate credentials against Erbium-maintained records.

When records conflict, the following order of authority normally applies:

1. the recognized Erbium relay record;
2. a stamped Settled Systems Continuity Registry record;
3. an approved medical or remembrance authorization;
4. a certified local government record;
5. an independently witnessed or off-stamp identity record.

The burden of resolving a discrepancy generally falls on the bearer.

Astrazoans are the population most frequently subjected to the provision because their bodies can pass conventional biometric screening. The statutory language remains ancestry-neutral, allowing it to be applied to:

- androids;
- heavily augmented persons;
- clones;
- reconstructed bodies;
- remembrances;
- transferred minds;
- persons affected by unknown biological or technological phenomena.

Erbium Industries interprets continuity as belonging to the registered legal person rather than to any particular appearance, established skin, service identity, or remembrance.

A registered skin may appear in the continuity record but does not receive independent legal standing.

## Interpretation of Closure Eligibility

Prosecutors must allege more than node interference to seek closure.

They must connect the continuity offense to intentional fatal harm, a killing, concealment of a killing, an attempted mass-casualty act, or another independently closure-eligible crime.

In practice, prosecutors often argue that interference with critical infrastructure inherently creates a substantial risk of death. This permits continuity offenses involving relays, ships, reactors, life-support systems, or medical facilities to be charged more severely than comparable fraud involving ordinary property.

Erbium Industries may investigate, detain, collect evidence, and recommend closure eligibility under delegated authority. The corporation may not formally impose closure without an authorized tribunal order.

# Procedures

## Routine Authentication

An authorized reader requests:

1. the node’s continuity key;
2. the node’s current integrity state;
3. confirmation from the recognized registry;
4. restrictions relevant to the requested service.

Routine systems are publicly described as receiving only the minimum information necessary to approve or deny access.

The bearer is not always informed:

- that a query occurred;
- which system performed it;
- what information was returned;
- how long the query record will be retained.

## Integrity Warning

When a node returns an integrity warning, the bearer may receive a temporary certificate allowing limited access while an inspection is scheduled.

Temporary certification may require:

- medical examination;
- comparison against prior body scans;
- genetic or tissue sampling;
- testimony from recognized witnesses;
- review of recent relay activity;
- review of registered skins or remembrances;
- surrender of the node for technical inspection.

## Unverified Continuity Hold

An authorized security officer may place a person under temporary hold when:

- the person requests access to critical infrastructure;
- the node returns a falsified or contradictory response;
- the person is suspected of carrying another person’s node;
- an active remembrance lacks authorization;
- the registry lists the bearer as dead, closed, missing, or already present elsewhere.

The officer must create a stamped incident record.

A routine continuity hold may continue for up to 1,440 standard minutes before judicial or administrative authorization is required for continued detention.

## Unverified Infiltrator Designation

An officer requesting an infiltrator designation must record the conduct supporting the allegation in addition to the failed continuity response.

The designation must identify:

- the infrastructure or identity allegedly targeted;
- the suspected method of concealment;
- the evidence of deliberate action;
- the immediate risk posed by release;
- whether a remembrance was involved.

## Closure Eligibility Referral

A prosecutor seeking closure eligibility must file a separate capital referral under Settled Systems Code `A6%8&1-3`.

The referral must identify:

- the alleged continuity violation;
- the aggravating act;
- the death, intended death, or mass-casualty risk involved;
- the evidence connecting the accused to that result;
- why noncapital prosecution is considered insufficient;
- whether the affected victims or families have been consulted.

Victim or family support is not legally required for a closure referral.

## Appeal

A person may challenge:

- unverified continuity status;
- infiltrator designation;
- detention;
- registry alteration;
- remembrance revocation;
- closure eligibility.

Filing an appeal does not automatically restore access, employment, travel eligibility, relay services, or release from detention.

Applicants are responsible for providing records sufficient to establish continuity. Off-stamp records may be accepted at the Registry’s discretion.

# Exemptions

Recognized exemptions include:

- medically necessary removal;
- authorized repair or replacement;
- temporary shielding during implantation or reimplantation;
- sealed intelligence operations;
- witness-protection identities;
- approved remembrance activity;
- authorized research;
- diplomatic concealment;
- locally approved off-stamp continuity protocols;
- emergency conditions in which verification equipment is unavailable.

Exemptions issued to intelligence personnel, corporate security, continuity personnel, and certain executives may allow their nodes to:

- return only limited confirmation;
- suppress location history;
- authenticate through sealed identities;
- avoid ordinary query logging;
- operate through classified remembrance credentials.

Ordinary employees rarely receive comparable protections.

# Penalties

## Accidental Damage or Administrative Noncompliance

Used when damage appears accidental or reporting requirements were not followed.

Possible consequences include:

- mandatory inspection;
- temporary continuity suspension;
- replacement or reimplantation costs;
- administrative fines;
- employment review;
- temporary removal from controlled duties;
- loss of remembrance authorization.

## Intentional Interference

Used when a person knowingly masks, removes, disables, or alters a node without an authorized variance.

Possible consequences include:

- detention;
- travel restriction;
- relay-service suspension;
- employment ineligibility;
- loss of corporate housing;
- service-credit reduction;
- fines;
- compulsory continuity evaluation;
- civic service;
- criminal prosecution.

## Falsification or Duplication

Used when a person produces or uses a false continuity response.

Possible consequences include:

- seizure of involved equipment;
- extended detention;
- asset freezing;
- continuity suspension;
- prosecution for impersonation;
- prosecution for infrastructure intrusion;
- unverified infiltrator designation;
- loss of remembrance authorization.

## Aggravated Continuity Interference

Used when continuity fraud involves controlled infrastructure, concealment of a death, evasion of custody, or the fraudulent exercise of another person’s authority.

Possible consequences include:

- long-term detention;
- permanent travel restrictions;
- permanent exclusion from relay-connected employment;
- seizure of relevant equipment and records;
- criminal prosecution under Administrative Title 6;
- referral for closure eligibility when the requirements of `A2%1&4-13.k` are met.

## Closure

Clause 13 does not itself impose closure.

Closure may be imposed only after:

1. conviction of a closure-eligible offense;
2. a separate sentencing determination;
3. confirmation by an authorized tribunal;
4. completion or exhaustion of the required review and appeal process;
5. issuance of a final closure order under Administrative Title 6.

Closure is execution.

# Practical Effect

Clause 13 allows an astrazoan to change every visible and measurable part of their body while remaining permanently readable to Erbium infrastructure.

Its practical effects include:

- connecting every registered skin to one corporate account;
- revealing that a person is astrazoan when their current form would otherwise conceal it;
- allowing employers to track registered service identities;
- confirming whether a remembrance is authorized;
- preventing an astrazoan from leaving an assigned identity behind without also leaving relay-connected society;
- turning node damage into immediate loss of work, housing, transportation, and records;
- assigning the cost of mandatory node replacement to the monitored employee;
- making prolonged off-stamp life appear suspicious;
- transforming identity verification into ancestry surveillance.

The provision addresses real risks. Astrazoan mimicry, remembrances, synthetic bodies, and advanced reconstruction can defeat ordinary biometric security.

The law distributes the burden of those risks almost entirely onto the people whose bodies make ordinary authentication unreliable.

# Public Controversy

Supporters argue that continuity nodes allow variable-form people to participate safely in a society built around legal identity, property, transportation, and restricted infrastructure.

Erbium propaganda claims that continuity nodes protect astrazoans from being blamed for crimes committed by someone using a similar appearance.

Critics argue that the provision:

- makes privacy conditional on bodily conformity;
- creates compulsory ancestry disclosure;
- treats refusal to be scanned as evidence of danger;
- gives Erbium control over whether a person can prove they exist;
- allows technical failure to suspend civil participation;
- places the financial cost of surveillance on the monitored person;
- permits secret exemptions for the institutions enforcing the law;
- makes Erbium-authorized remembrances legal while criminalizing independent uses of the same biology.

Astrazoan advocacy groups commonly summarize the law as:

> Every skin is permitted so long as the company can see beneath it.

## Controversy Surrounding Closure

Settled Systems authorities describe closure as a final resolution for victims, families, institutions, and communities affected by severe crimes.

Prosecutors frequently argue that closure:

- prevents continuing danger;
- spares communities from indefinite uncertainty;
- confirms the finality of a conviction;
- allows survivors to proceed without further exposure to the offender;
- restores trust in essential infrastructure.

Critics reject the claim that execution reliably produces healing or finality. They argue that closure proceedings extend cases through repeated reviews, force victims and families to relive the offense, and use their grief to justify a punishment they may not support.

Some victims’ families publicly demand closure. Others oppose the execution of the convicted person or resent being treated as though their recovery depends upon it.

The law does not require family support before prosecutors seek closure.

# GM Information

> [!danger] GM Only
> Clause 13 began as a narrowly written anti-fraud rule. Erbium Industries supported amendments that transformed it into a general authority over variable-form identity.

## The Publicized Incident

The law’s expansion was justified through a highly publicized remembrance-related security breach.

Public reports claimed that an unauthorized astrazoan assumed the identity of a deceased relay technician, entered a restricted facility, and nearly caused a system-wide communications failure.

The sealed investigation showed that:

- the astrazoan had previously been employed as an authorized remembrance;
- Erbium failed to revoke the original access credentials;
- the relay failure was caused by deferred maintenance;
- the astrazoan entered the facility attempting to prevent the failure;
- no evidence established an intent to impersonate the technician for personal gain.

Erbium used the incident to shift responsibility from obsolete infrastructure and negligent credential management to astrazoan biology.

## Registry Backdoor

Continuity nodes publicly transmit a limited identity key.

Erbium’s technical systems can retrieve substantially more information than ordinary users are told, including:

- recent query locations;
- registered skins;
- remembrance assignments;
- employment status;
- access attempts;
- medical integrity warnings;
- off-stamp intervals;
- security classifications;
- unresolved identity flags;
- pending closure referrals.

This information is retained longer than public policy states.

## Manufactured Discrepancies

Authorized registry administrators can mark a valid continuity key as:

- compromised;
- duplicated;
- under review;
- associated with a deceased person;
- subject to detention;
- technically noncompliant;
- referred for closure eligibility.

The physical node may remain fully functional while every connected system reports an identity discrepancy.

This allows Erbium to manufacture the appearance of node tampering without touching the person’s body.

The resulting process is called **continuity suspension**, not closure. The person remains alive while losing practical access to housing, employment, transportation, medical records, and financial accounts.

## Sealed Variances

Erbium intelligence, corporate security, continuity personnel, and certain executives routinely receive variances allowing their nodes to:

- suppress location data;
- return incomplete identity information;
- authenticate through alternate identities;
- avoid ordinary query logging;
- continue functioning through sealed remembrance credentials.

The company publicly describes equivalent behavior by ordinary astrazoans as an intolerable threat to identity integrity.

## Closure Charging Practices

Erbium prosecutors cannot impose closure directly, but they use closure eligibility as leverage.

A defendant accused of entering a relay station with a masked node may be told that prosecutors will pursue closure on the theory that the act created a mass-casualty risk unless the defendant:

- accepts a lesser conviction;
- identifies accomplices;
- surrenders off-stamp contacts;
- relinquishes remembrance records;
- waives claims against Erbium;
- enters long-term civic service.

The resulting plea is publicly recorded as evidence that the continuity system successfully prevented a closure-eligible threat.

## Victims and Institutional Finality

Closure proceedings are presented as being conducted for victims and affected families.

Internally, Erbium values closure because an execution can:

- prevent further testimony;
- end appeals over restricted evidence;
- terminate disputes concerning continuity records;
- prevent the accused from exposing registry manipulation;
- demonstrate control over threats to corporate infrastructure.

Families who oppose closure may be excluded from public messaging or described as unable to appreciate the broader needs of the Settled Systems.

## The Capital Threshold Is Deliberately Elastic

The phrase **substantial and imminent risk of multiple deaths** is interpreted aggressively when continuity fraud involves Erbium infrastructure.

A masked node near an ordinary warehouse is treated as property crime.

The same masked node near a relay, ship, reactor, clinic, atmosphere processor, or extraction-control center may be presented as evidence of an intended mass-casualty act even when no sabotage occurred.

This allows Erbium to reserve its most severe punishment for violations committed in places where its authority is most materially important.
