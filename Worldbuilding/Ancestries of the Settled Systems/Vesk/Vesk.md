![[ancestryPortrait_Vesk.png#wrapright|300]]Vesk are heavily built reptilian humanoids native to [[Zakaron]], where physical strength has served as weapon, civic obligation, political credential, and family inheritance. Their holds fought throughout the ancient [[Zakaron#The Inversion|Rupture Wars]], contributing to the destruction of the planetary atmosphere and the collapse that forced Zakaron’s surviving peoples to rebuild civilization along the inside of the crust.

The same institutions that helped destroy the planet became essential to its survival. Vesk holds supplied soldiers, emergency labor, structural engineers, heavy equipment, and disciplined workforces during the construction of [[Zakaron#Kharosai|Kharosai]]. They defended pressure systems, stabilized damaged districts, transported entire populations, and held critical infrastructure while artificial gravity, atmosphere, and the [[Zakaron#The Sunlock|Sunlock]] remained unreliable.

This history produced a culture that places enormous value on visible responsibility. A vesk is expected to stand where they have committed to stand, carry burdens within their capability, protect those placed under their authority, and answer publicly for the force they use. Strength earns respect because other people can build plans around it.

The same ideal allows institutions to claim a vesk’s body, labor, courage, and violence as communal resources. Holds assign duties according to ability, rank, lineage, and political need. Employers assume vesk workers can tolerate greater danger. Commanders treat endurance as consent to continued deployment. Refusal may be judged as cowardice, disloyalty, or failure to carry one’s share.

When the [[The Star Dream|Star Dream]] reached Zakaron, many vesk interpreted it as a revelation of worlds awaiting conquest. Vesk assault forces led the War of the Open Stars while Madrosarai operatives sabotaged defenses, disrupted command systems, and prepared targets for occupation. Zakaron won enough early victories to become a lasting military threat, then exhausted the erbium deposits that sustained its fleets.

The [[Zakaron#The Still-Drive Accords|Still-Drive Accords]] ended the expansion and established the beginning of Post-Erbium Standard. Zakaron retained its government, military, industries, and internal sovereignty while [[Erbium Industries LLC]] gained decisive influence over its access to refined erbium, Drift components, and recognized relay infrastructure.

More than nine centuries later, vesk communities exist throughout the [[Settled Systems]]. Zakaron-born vesk may retain direct obligations to a hold, familiarity with military institutions, and strong political opinions regarding Erbium. Offworld vesk may possess only distant family stories, ceremonial affiliations, or no meaningful relationship with Zakaron at all.

Vesk remain strongly associated with soldiers, security personnel, heavy industry, emergency response, and command. Many embrace those roles. Others become physicians, artists, engineers, negotiators, caregivers, scholars, organizers, or workers whose lives have little connection to warfare. Every vesk still encounters expectations created by a body and ancestry that other people associate with force.

If you want to roleplay a physically formidable character deciding which duties deserve their strength, who has the authority to command it, and whether honor can survive service to an unjust institution, you should play a vesk.

# You Might...

- Treat an oath as a practical commitment that other people should be able to plan their lives around.
- Step into danger first because you possess the ability, because others expect it, or because you refuse to let someone weaker carry the risk.
- Judge leaders by whether they accept visible responsibility for the consequences of their orders.
- Feel pride in Zakaron’s survival while carrying anger, shame, or admiration regarding its history of conquest.
- Challenge an authority directly when private obedience would allow that authority to avoid accountability.

# Others Probably...

- Assume you possess military training, security credentials, or a willingness to use violence.
- Expect you to carry heavy equipment, withstand injury, or enter dangerous areas regardless of your actual occupation.
- Interpret your size, scales, horns, and direct speech as threatening before learning your intentions.
- Treat emotional restraint as proof of stoicism and emotional expression as evidence that you have lost control.
- Hold you personally responsible for Zakaron’s military history or view you as a natural ally against Erbium.

# Physical Description
Vesk are tall, dense, muscular humanoids covered in thick scales. Most adults stand between 6 and 7 feet tall and possess broad shoulders, powerful limbs, heavy torsos, and long muscular tails. Their weight often exceeds that of similarly sized humanoids because of dense bone, layered musculature, and protective scales.

Their scales usually appear in shades of green, gray, blue, brown, black, ocher, or muted red. Mottled patterns, contrasting throats, bright facial patches, and bands of color along the limbs or tail are common. Some families cultivate vivid scale patterns through selective inheritance, medical treatment, or cosmetic alteration.

Vesk heads possess heavy jaws, pronounced brows, and rows of sharp teeth suited to an omnivorous predator. Horns, cranial spines, and bony ridges grow from the skull and jaw. Dense projections beneath the lower jaw often form a natural beardlike structure. Some vesk grow additional spines along the shoulders, back, elbows, or tail.

Horns and scales continue growing throughout much of a vesk’s life. Regular grooming includes filing damaged edges, polishing healthy scales, treating cracks, and removing dead tissue around the claws. Many vesk paint, stain, engrave, cap, or decorate their horns and scales to identify family, hold, military service, profession, mourning status, or personal style.

The hands end in thick claws capable of tearing softer materials and providing traction on industrial surfaces. Vesk feet are broad and stable, with reinforced toes suited to bearing weight and maintaining footing during impact. Their tails aid balance, communicate tension and attention, and can support equipment adapted for tail use.

Vesk possess substantial physical durability. Their dense muscles and skeletons support heavy armor, integrated weapons, industrial tools, and cybernetic systems that would place severe stress on lighter bodies. Their ordinary movement is deliberate and somewhat slow, though they can produce considerable force over short distances.

Pre-Inversion vesk populations developed across deserts, swamps, forests, coastlines, caves, mountains, and heavily contested urban regions. Modern vesk retain wide biological variation from these environments. Some possess venomous saliva, heat-regulating frills, climbing tails, aquatic adaptations, heightened night vision, or unusually dense natural armor.

Zakaron’s medical and military programs have intensified many inherited traits. A lineage associated with hazardous engineering might receive skeletal reinforcement across generations. A military hold may cultivate thicker scales or greater heat tolerance. An aquatic lineage may preserve gills and webbing even after living in Kharosai for centuries.

Cybernetics are common among Zakaron vesk. Industrial workers use reinforced joints, spinal load distributors, artificial organs, and vestibular systems that protect against gravity faults. Soldiers integrate armor controls, targeting systems, weapon mounts, and encrypted communications directly into their bodies.

Visible augmentation carries little inherent stigma. Vesk usually judge an implant by its function, reliability, cost, and the authority controlling it. An advanced military system may represent prestige, permanent service obligation, or a remote safeguard capable of restricting its user.

# Society
Vesk society on Zakaron is organized through **holds**. A hold can function as an extended family, territorial government, military command, industrial consortium, school, mutual-defense network, or combination of these institutions.

Membership may come through birth, adoption, marriage, oath, military service, employment, political alliance, or the formal acceptance of responsibility for another person. Some holds contain millions of members and control large districts of Kharosai. Others consist of a few families maintaining a workshop, vessel, neighborhood, or inherited obligation.

A hold offers housing, education, medical support, employment contacts, legal protection, military defense, and assistance during infrastructure emergencies. In return, members owe service according to their training, rank, resources, and physical ability.

The relationship can provide genuine security. A worker injured during a gravity collapse may receive care from their hold after an employer ends their contract. A family facing retaliation from another institution can invoke the hold’s protection. Children gain access to training, mentors, and a community prepared to defend them.

Hold authority can also reach deeply into private life. Leaders assign careers, approve transfers, negotiate marriages, direct military service, control specialized cybernetics, and decide which conflicts require a member’s participation. A person with rare strength, technical aptitude, or inherited adaptation may be treated as a resource whose life choices affect the entire hold.

The major holds are represented in the [[Zakaron#The Zakaron Concord|Assembly of Holds]]. Representation depends on population, industrial capacity, territorial responsibility, fleet contribution, and inherited agreements dating to the reconstruction of Kharosai.

The Assembly controls defense appropriations, heavy infrastructure, civil mobilization, fleet construction, and much of Zakaron’s industrial policy. Its figurehead is the **Kharduun**, commonly translated as **High Marshal**. The older meaning of the title is “the one who carries the wall.”

The Kharduun shares executive authority with the Madrosarai Saivara through the [[Zakaron#The Zakaron Concord|Paired Mandate]]. The Kharduun oversees visible force, planetary defense, emergency deployment, and public military command. The Saivara oversees intelligence, classified research, cyberwarfare, and covert security.

Vesk and Madrosarai daily life is thoroughly integrated. They share neighborhoods, factories, military units, universities, criminal organizations, political coalitions, and family networks. Holds and blood-clans maintain formal compacts involving territory, production, medical support, defense, and retaliation.

The two political traditions place value on different methods. Vesk institutions emphasize declared purpose, visible commitment, direct command, and public responsibility. Madrosarai institutions emphasize controlled information, precise intervention, hidden preparation, and protection of sensitive knowledge.

During war, these approaches can operate with extraordinary efficiency. During internal conflict, vesk accuse Madrosarai clans of manipulating events while avoiding responsibility. Madrosarai accuse vesk holds of confusing visibility with honesty and directness with sound judgment.

Vesk culture treats conflict as an ordinary part of social life. Disputes may be resolved through formal debate, physical contest, competitive labor, arbitration, tactical games, public challenge, or controlled combat. The chosen form depends on the stakes and the traditions of the participating holds.

A challenge establishes that the disagreement deserves recognition. Accepting the challenge creates obligations regarding conduct, witnesses, consequences, and the conditions under which the dispute ends. Violence outside an accepted structure is judged according to the harm caused and the responsibility accepted afterward.

Vesk pursue status through demonstrated capability. Military service remains prestigious, though mastery in engineering, medicine, law, art, logistics, education, or public administration can carry equal honor within the appropriate hold. A respected person is someone whose presence allows others to rely upon a difficult task being completed.

This standard creates intense pressure to remain useful. Injury, disability, age, exhaustion, or refusal of service can threaten a person’s standing. Some holds treat care for weakened members as proof of collective strength. Others retain people only while their skills remain valuable.

Vesk who sever their hold affiliations are commonly called **Unheld**. Some leave because they reject an assignment, political alliance, marriage agreement, military order, or inherited debt. Others lose membership through expulsion, criminal judgment, surrender, or conflict between competing holds.

Unheld vesk gain independence from formal command while losing many systems of protection. Zakaron reformers have created independent housing, medical networks, legal associations, and labor organizations for them. Erbium recruits heavily among Unheld populations, offering offworld transport and employment in exchange for commitments that often reproduce the same dependency under corporate authority.

Offworld vesk society varies widely. Some diaspora communities maintain formal hold structures adapted to local law. Others use the hold as a cultural association, family network, military society, union, business cooperative, or ceremonial affiliation.

Many vesk raised away from Zakaron understand honor through their local community rather than through ancestral institutions. A vesk may serve a mining cooperative, hospital, frontier settlement, religious group, ship crew, or family with the same seriousness that a Zakaron traditionalist gives to a hold.

# Beliefs
Vesk beliefs emphasize the relationship between capability, commitment, and consequence. Strength carries meaning when someone uses it deliberately and accepts responsibility for what follows.

An oath is valuable because other people reorganize their choices around it. A vesk who promises to guard a district, support a family, complete a repair, or defend a subordinate creates expectations that may determine whether others survive. Breaking the oath transfers the resulting danger to people who relied upon it.

Vesk therefore distinguish between failure and abandonment. A person may fail after exhausting their ability, misjudging a situation, or confronting circumstances beyond their preparation. Abandonment occurs when a person discards a responsibility while retaining the capacity to act.

Traditional holds teach that authority flows from accepted burden. A commander deserves obedience because the commander carries responsibility for the unit, the mission, and the consequences of every order. Vesk political conflicts frequently begin when subordinates believe leadership has retained authority while transferring every risk downward.

The **carried wall** is a common metaphor in vesk philosophy. A wall protects the people behind it while bearing pressure from outside. Every community depends on people willing to carry part of that weight. The title Kharduun reflects this ideal.

The metaphor also supports authoritarian politics. Hold leaders frequently describe obedience, sacrifice, secrecy, and continued labor as portions of the wall each person must carry. Critics answer that some leaders stand safely behind the structure while assigning its entire weight to others.

Honor requires visible ownership of action. Many vesk consider anonymous violence, concealed command, and responsibility dispersed through bureaucracy morally dangerous. A person issuing an order should attach their name, rank, and authority to it.

This principle creates tension with Madrosarai veilwork and Erbium administration. Both can distribute decisions across classified records, automated systems, legal departments, and chains of authorization until accountability becomes difficult to locate.

Vesk attitudes toward conquest remain divided. Traditional expansionists believe powerful institutions have an obligation to impose order where existing authorities produce instability. The War of the Open Stars drew heavily from this belief.

Reformers argue that the Rupture Wars and the destruction of Zakaron demonstrate the consequences of treating military capacity as proof of moral authority. They define strength through restraint, defense, rebuilding, and willingness to protect people from one’s own institutions.

Sunlock stewardship appears throughout Zakaron vesk traditions. Every district depends on artificial gravity, atmosphere, containment, and power. Service to these systems carries religious, civic, and political significance. Sabotage against shared survival infrastructure is among the most serious possible offenses.

Vesk ancestor practices focus on remembered actions. Families preserve weapons, tools, armor plates, engineering records, oath tablets, service histories, and accounts of consequential decisions. An ancestor’s failures may be recorded alongside achievements because later generations are expected to understand the full weight of what they inherited.

**Popular Edicts** keep commitments others rely upon, protect people placed under your authority, accept responsibility for the force you use, challenge unworthy leadership openly, prepare yourself for difficult service, preserve infrastructure that sustains the community

**Popular Anathema** abandon dependents while capable of aiding them, order others into danger while concealing your authority, demand sacrifice you refuse to share, use superior strength as proof of moral correctness, endanger shared infrastructure for private advantage, claim credit while transferring blame to subordinates

Common vesk names include Abazobari, Ahadigar, Asthonad, Dotralan, Evdokayo, Julukesh, Katara, Obozaya, Oromeras, Radokama, Sarangari, Sobok, Terikoraz, Vindasorn, and Yuluzak.

Vesk often shorten formal names outside Zakaron. Diaspora communities may combine Vesk names with local family names, occupational titles, ship names, or settlement affiliations.

# Other Information

## Vesk Holds
A hold begins with a declared responsibility. Ancient holds formed around fortified settlements, military forces, water systems, food stores, industrial districts, transportation routes, or the protection of particular families.

During the Inversion, holds that controlled shelters, machinery, troops, and surviving supply routes became the foundations of reconstruction government. Their agreements determined which populations received pressure space, gravity coverage, heat, food, and protection.

Modern holds preserve this history through charters describing the responsibilities they claim and the service they expect from members. A hold controlling a shipyard may promise employment, defense, technical education, and emergency shelter. Members may owe labor quotas, military readiness, political support, and participation in collective debt.

Large holds contain several internal branches. One may operate factories, another train soldiers, another administer housing, and another negotiate with Madrosarai blood-clans. Rival branches regularly compete over resources and interpretation of the hold’s purpose.

A member can challenge a hold leader through procedures established in the charter. These challenges may involve public argument, evidence of failed responsibility, a contest recognized by the hold, or support from a sufficient portion of its membership.

Powerful holds shape the challenge rules to protect established leadership. A worker may possess a theoretical right to challenge an industrial commander while lacking the money, witnesses, legal access, medical clearance, or free time required to complete the process.

## The Rupture Wars
The Rupture Wars lasted across generations and involved shifting coalitions of vesk holds and Madrosarai blood-clans. Most factions claimed that control of planetary infrastructure was necessary to prevent rivals from using it against them.

Vesk forces conducted territorial invasions, siege operations, mass mobilizations, and assaults against atmosphere processors, orbital defenses, mantle facilities, and population shelters. Every escalation created a new justification for further militarization.

Some holds attempted to limit the destruction. Others developed weapons specifically designed to make strategic regions uninhabitable. Several modern political families derive their authority from the forces that survived these campaigns.

The Inversion transformed military defeat into planetary collapse. Vesk traditions commonly commemorate the holds that defended evacuation corridors, stabilized shelters, and supplied reconstruction labor. Records of the holds that caused the damage receive less public attention.

Modern reformers argue that survival service cannot erase responsibility for the catastrophe. Traditionalists answer that present generations inherit obligations to preserve Zakaron rather than guilt for decisions made before their birth.

## The War of the Open Stars
The Star Dream gave Zakaron access to intersystem travel while its military institutions remained organized around expansion, readiness, and competition between holds.

Many vesk leaders interpreted the Dream as proof that Zakaron had survived its trials to bring disciplined order to weaker worlds. The War of the Open Stars began with demands for submission, access, tribute, relay rights, and military passage.

Vesk forces specialized in boarding actions, station seizure, planetary assault, urban occupation, and defense of captured infrastructure. Their armor, weapons, cybernetics, and coordination exceeded those available to many early Elect societies.

Madrosarai veil agents prepared campaigns through sabotage, assassination, intercepted communications, and manipulation of local political conflicts. Vesk units then became the visible face of conquest and carried most occupation duties.

The campaign expanded faster than Zakaron’s erbium reserves. Other bodies in the system were mined until accessible deposits became exhausted. Warships remained armed and operational while losing reliable access to Drift cores and relay components.

Erbium and its allied governments used supply access as leverage. The Still-Drive Accords preserved Zakaron’s internal sovereignty while restricting fleet deployment and connecting interstellar mobility to Erbium-controlled erbium allocations.

Modern vesk disagree over the war’s meaning. Revanchists regard the Accords as a humiliation imposed through resource monopoly. Reformers regard the war as a second warning against equating superior force with legitimate rule. Many diaspora vesk see both positions as arguments belonging to a homeland they have never visited.

## Armor and the Vesk Body
Vesk military and industrial design treats the body as a structural platform. Armor attaches across the shoulders, spine, hips, and tail rather than hanging primarily from the torso. Weight is distributed through reinforced joints and dense musculature.

Zakaron armor commonly includes removable plates, mechanical supports, integrated medical systems, environmental sealing, tool mounts, and direct connections to cybernetic interfaces. Military suits can become extensions of the wearer’s nervous system.

Some vesk possess scales dense enough to function as natural armor. These lineages may etch protective runes, install subdermal reinforcement, or integrate hard points directly into their bodies. Others prefer external armor that can be removed, repaired, exchanged, and separated from personal identity.

Hold ownership complicates the relationship between wearer and equipment. A soldier may receive extensive augmentation and armor through service, while the hold retains ownership of the control systems, maintenance tools, encryption keys, and replacement components.

Leaving service can mean surrendering equipment integrated into the body. Some holds provide safe removal and civilian replacements. Others classify continued possession as theft of military property.

Erbium contracts use similar structures for offworld vesk security personnel. Corporate equipment agreements may treat armor interfaces, reinforced organs, combat prosthetics, and targeting implants as leased service assets.

## Vesk Beyond Zakaron
![[ancestryPortrait_Vesk2.png#wrapright|300]]
Vesk migration began during and after the War of the Open Stars. Some communities originated as occupation forces that remained after the peace. Others formed around prisoners, diplomats, merchants, technicians, military exchanges, political exiles, and workers recruited into Settled Systems industries.

Nine centuries of migration have produced communities with their own traditions. A vesk family on Agrio Dysis IV may define honor through ranch protection and water agreements. A Theosis-born vesk may inherit a corporate security career, reject it, or organize pressure workers against the same institution that employed their parents.

Zakaron’s government recognizes many diaspora holds and encourages ceremonial loyalty to the Concord. It also claims an interest in rare biological adaptations, classified military training, and descendants of politically significant lineages.

Diaspora movements increasingly reject the idea that ancestry or inherited hold records create automatic political obligations. Some petition Zakaron to release their affiliations. Others preserve hold membership while demanding equal authority over its policies.

## Place in the Settled Systems
Vesk work throughout military service, corporate security, emergency response, heavy construction, industrial repair, freight handling, hazardous extraction, law enforcement, athletics, and command.

Their physical durability and reputation for duty make them attractive to employers assigning dangerous work. A vesk may be expected to remain during an evacuation, restrain an unstable worker, carry injured personnel, or absorb violence before other employees enter danger.

Erbium describes these expectations as leadership opportunities and role-appropriate deployment. Refusal can affect promotion, security clearance, housing access, and assessments of command reliability.

Vesk also occupy positions of real authority. Erbium security divisions recruit Zakaron-trained officers, purchase licensed weapons, and adopt hold-derived command practices. Some vesk commanders enforce corporate policy with conviction, believing order protects workers from greater instability.

Others use military organization against Erbium. Vesk labor leaders create disciplined strike defenses, evacuation plans, supply networks, and mutual-protection groups. Their ability to organize visible resistance makes them valuable allies and prominent targets.

Zakaron remains the strongest planetary government positioned to challenge Erbium directly. Its motives remain divided between sovereignty, commercial leverage, political reform, and renewed expansion. Vesk throughout the Settled Systems therefore encounter two competing stereotypes: agents of authoritarian conquest and natural leaders of anti-corporate resistance.

Vesk serving in the Frontier Extraction Division commonly work as security personnel, heavy-equipment operators, rescue specialists, armor technicians, demolition workers, pilots, expedition leaders, and hazardous-environment laborers. Some accept frontier service as an honorable responsibility. Others use the assignment to leave a hold, escape corporate command, acquire independent resources, or decide for themselves what their strength will protect.
