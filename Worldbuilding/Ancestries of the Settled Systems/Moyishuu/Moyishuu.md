The Moyishuu awoke from a dream.

Before they possessed bodies, the beings that would become Moyishuu existed as diffuse awareness within clouds of cosmic dust. They drifted through the cold distances between worlds, collecting around stellar nurseries and watching immense structures of gas, pressure, gravity, and heat collapse into new stars. These places remain within Moyishuu memory as the **Star Forges**.

The [[The Star Dream|Star Dream]] passed through this awareness long before any Moyishuu possessed a face, voice, or individual name. It brought images of inhabited worlds, distant peoples, advanced machinery, and the hyperspatial plane that would become known as the [[The Drift|Drift]]. Moyishuu traditions consequently claim that their formless ancestors received the Dream before any embodied civilization. The Drift eventually pressed against the universe and drew portions of the Moyishuu awareness into its currents. Passage through that plane compressed vast perceptions into finite minds. Cosmic dust, fey substance, Drift energy, and patterns carried through the Star Dream gathered into stable humanoid bodies.

The first embodied Moyishuu emerged from the Drift as distinct people. Each possessed a personal mind, a limited memory, and a body that moved through time in one direction. The knowledge accumulated during their formless existence could not fit within their new brains. Most of it disappeared into fragmented dreams of stars, nebulae, and celestial events whose locations and dates remain impossible to verify.

Moyishuu call their formless existence the **First Dream** and embodied life the **Second Dream**.

Modern Moyishuu are born through sexual reproduction, pregnancy, and childbirth. Conception occurs only within the Drift, where its energies activate the biological and crystalline processes required for a new life to begin. Once conception has occurred, pregnancy can continue aboard a vessel, on a station, or within realspace. Every generation has become more physically stable and more closely bound to material existence. Modern Moyishuu possess ordinary organs, recognizable lifespans, and bodies capable of living among the other peoples of the [[Settled Systems]]. They still survive in vacuum, hover above the ground, sense cosmic beings, and act as living connections to the Drift.

They also inherit dreams of experiences they never personally lived. A Moyishuu might remember the pressure inside a forming star, a nebula viewed across thousands of years, or the moment a distant world first acquired oceans. These memories arrive without reliable sequence, explanation, or practical knowledge.

Moyishuu spend much of their lives aboard traveling communities called **constellations**. They visit planets eagerly, form intense relationships, join local projects, perform for unfamiliar audiences, and seek experiences unavailable anywhere else. Most eventually return to space, where the open distance between worlds feels closer to the environment from which their ancestry emerged.

Their natural connection to the Drift places them in direct conflict with [[Erbium Industries LLC]]. A Moyishuu can allow a vessel without a Drift engine to enter or leave hyperspace, providing emergency mobility beyond Erbium’s erbium-dependent machinery. The vessel must still travel using conventional thrusters, making the passage slow and difficult, but the ability remains valuable to isolated settlements, off-stamp crews, smugglers, rescue teams, and anyone denied corporate transportation.

Erbium recruits Moyishuu as pilots, navigators, emergency transit specialists, relay surveyors, and living engine contingencies. It also controls many of the registered vessels, ports, medical facilities, and scheduled crossings used by Moyishuu families to conceive children.

If you want to roleplay a cosmic traveler building a finite identity through places, relationships, art, and experience while institutions attempt to regulate their connection to interstellar travel, you should play a moyishuu.

# Physical Description

Moyishuu are slender humanoid fey who generally stand between 4 and 6 feet tall. Their height, build, and facial structure vary considerably, though long limbs, narrow features, pointed ears, and flexible joints are common. They mature at approximately the same rate as humans and can live for as long as 200 years.

Their skin appears in saturated shades rarely found among ordinary humanoids, including violet, indigo, magenta, cyan, blue, green, copper, rose, and combinations of several colors. Fine mineral particles suspended within their tissues produce a soft sheen under direct light. The effect resembles light passing through dust-rich atmosphere rather than a constant glow.

Dark markings cross most Moyishuu bodies. These patterns can resemble branching veins, cracked crystal, Drift currents, nebular boundaries, or the shadows between clouds of cosmic matter. They commonly extend along the limbs, spine, face, throat, and torso. The markings slowly change throughout a Moyishuu’s life. Prolonged Drift travel can deepen a line, split one pattern into several branches, or cause a new shape to appear. Moyishuu sometimes compare old portraits to their current bodies as a record of accumulated travel, though the markings provide no reliable map or chronology.

A Moyishuu’s eyes often possess dark sclera surrounding bright irises of silver, violet, green, blue, or gold. Tiny reflective structures within the iris can resemble distant points of light. Their vision otherwise functions within ordinary humanoid limits.


Moyishuu hair consists of a living crystalline symbiote that grows from the scalp in branching filaments, angular locks, plates, soft mineral fibers, or clusters resembling translucent crystal. The symbiote formed alongside the bodies of the first embodied Moyishuu and now passes naturally from parent to child. Crystalline hair can be trimmed, polished, carved, colored through mineral intake, or guided into new growth patterns. Damaged sections slowly regrow. Many Moyishuu incorporate jewelry, communication hardware, sensor contacts, lights, or tiny resonating instruments into the crystal. The crystal responds subtly to bodily condition. Fatigue can make it cloudy. Illness can produce uneven growth or brittle edges. Excitement, physical exertion, and proximity to active Drift phenomena may increase its translucence or produce faint internal light.

Moyishuu possess male and female sexes, and their cultures generally recognize male and female as the ancestry’s two genders. Physical differences between them are moderate and vary between families. Clothing, jewelry, crystal styling, occupation, and personal behavior remain highly expressive within that binary structure.

Moyishuu bodies retain an incomplete attachment to ordinary gravity. They can walk normally, though many hover slightly when turning, resting, crossing a gap, or becoming distracted. Clothing uses fitted pockets, straps, magnetic closures, and tether points to prevent loose belongings from drifting away. Their natural flight is slow and controlled. A Moyishuu may rise to reach a high shelf, cross broken terrain, work along a ship’s ceiling, or remain suspended while sleeping. Most cannot achieve the speed or maneuverability of a dedicated flying creature without training.

In vacuum, a Moyishuu becomes cooler, more translucent, and slightly brighter as the cosmic material within their body responds to the surrounding environment. Their crystalline hair spreads more freely without gravity, and their dark markings become easier to see. They can survive without breathing, though speech in vacuum still requires radio, tactile signals, or another medium capable of carrying communication.

Modern Moyishuu require food, water, rest, and ordinary medical care. Their cosmic origin provides several powerful adaptations without freeing them from bodily needs. Injuries can fracture crystalline tissue, disrupt flight, or interfere with their Drift connection.

# Society
![[plane_Drift.png#center]]


Moyishuu possess no ancestral homeworld. Their largest communities live aboard starships, mobile habitats, Drift stations, and connected fleets called **constellations**.

A constellation can include several ships, extended families, close friends, partners, apprentices, dependents, and long-term crewmates. Its vessels may travel together for years, separate across multiple systems, and reunite when routes allow.

Membership develops through birth, marriage, adoption, apprenticeship, shared travel, rescue, or personal commitment. A Moyishuu can belong to more than one constellation, though every membership carries specific obligations involving transport, childcare, maintenance, medical support, communication, or emergency aid.

Larger constellation ships function as mobile towns. They contain residential decks, machine shops, classrooms, performance halls, medical facilities, conception suites, hydroponic gardens, observation chambers, archives, and docking structures for smaller vessels.

Moyishuu ships are frequently old. Their interiors show layers of modification accumulated through generations of owners and crews. A corridor may combine pressure panels from three manufacturers, wall art from several systems, handwritten maintenance notes, a temporary stage, and a viewing port cut into a section never designed to hold one.

The ships remain functional through constant maintenance. Moyishuu affection for novelty can produce experimental modifications, though every successful constellation develops people willing to inspect seals, replace filters, catalogue parts, and prevent an expressive redesign from interfering with life support.

Moyishuu travel to experience particular places rather than to collect destinations. A constellation might remain near one world for several years while its members study an ecosystem, participate in a political movement, operate a business, learn a local art, or build relationships. Once the experience no longer requires their presence, many move on.

Some Moyishuu settle permanently on planets. They establish families, farms, workshops, theaters, laboratories, and ordinary careers. Planetary Moyishuu often remain connected to a constellation through visits, correspondence, shared childcare, or participation in conception voyages.

Many Moyishuu describe planetary life as crowded despite the physical space available. A horizon, atmosphere, and fixed gravitational direction can create a sense of enclosure in people accustomed to seeing stars from every side of a vessel.

Moyishuu culture treats personal experience as the material from which identity is constructed. The First Dream contained immense perception without distinct ownership. The Second Dream allows an event to happen to one specific person and become part of that person’s history.

This belief encourages intense participation. Moyishuu frequently immerse themselves in a profession, relationship, performance, political cause, or hobby for a limited period. Ending that involvement does not automatically make it insincere. The experience remains part of the person after the activity has ended.

Temporary bands, short-lived businesses, rotating crews, and project-specific households are common. Some last for a single performance or voyage. Others continue across generations because the people involved repeatedly choose to renew them.

Moyishuu partnerships can be lifelong or established for a defined journey, project, or period of time. A fixed-term bond carries serious obligations while active. Its expiration requires an explicit decision to renew, renegotiate, or separate.

Children are usually raised by parents alongside a wider constellation. Ship crews share instruction, supervision, and practical training. Young Moyishuu learn vacuum safety, tether use, basic maintenance, constellation history, and methods for recording dreams before receiving responsibility aboard a vessel.

Every Moyishuu begins dreaming of stars during childhood. Families encourage children to describe these dreams without claiming that every image carries prophecy. Some dreams correspond to known astronomical structures. Others contain impossible geometries, stars in the wrong stage of development, or events recorded long before the child’s birth.

Constellations maintain extensive dream archives. These include drawings, songs, sensor-assisted sleep recordings, oral accounts, and comparisons between several generations. Large collections sometimes reveal recurring structures or events that no individual dream described clearly.

The Moyishuu relationship with authority changes according to the size and purpose of a constellation. Small ships rely on captains or crew councils. Large vessels maintain elected administrators, technical officers, rotating judges, and emergency authorities.

Leadership is usually tied to function. A pilot controls navigation while underway. An engineer can halt operations when machinery becomes unsafe. A medical officer controls quarantine and treatment spaces. Constellation assemblies address decisions that exceed one role’s authority.

This system can become disorderly when several specialists claim overlapping jurisdiction. Charismatic Moyishuu can dominate a meeting through performance and personal influence. Long-lived captains can accumulate enough loyalty to turn an elected position into practical ownership.

Constellations carry the same political divisions found among other peoples. Wealthy ships control valuable routes, advanced engines, and desirable conception facilities. Poor constellations work freight contracts, lease equipment, accept dangerous surveys, and postpone maintenance until failure becomes unavoidable.

Access to the Drift creates the deepest material division. Every Moyishuu possesses the ability to connect a vessel to hyperspace, but a ship traveling through the Drift on conventional thrusters moves slowly. Fast, predictable interstellar movement still depends on erbium-powered engines, reliable navigation data, and relay access.

Erbium Industries uses this limitation to recruit and control constellations. It offers engine access, maintenance contracts, reproductive crossings, medical facilities, and preferred routes in exchange for labor, data, exclusivity, and continuing service.

Moyishuu communities respond in several ways. Some cooperate because corporate access allows their constellations to travel farther and support larger populations. Others maintain old engines independently, travel slowly through unregistered routes, or coordinate several Drift Eikons to support off-stamp migration.

# Beliefs

Moyishuu beliefs begin with the division between the First Dream and the Second Dream.

The First Dream describes existence before personhood. The earliest Moyishuu possessed vast awareness without individual bodies, private memories, family relationships, or personal choices. They observed the cosmos across immense periods while lacking a distinct self capable of claiming any one experience.

The Second Dream is the life of an embodied Moyishuu. It begins with conception in the Drift and continues through childhood, travel, relationships, work, creation, failure, aging, and death.

Many Moyishuu consider the Second Dream more valuable because its limits allow experiences to belong to someone. A meal shared with one friend, a song written during one voyage, or a repair completed while afraid carries meaning through its specificity.

Other Moyishuu believe embodiment represents a temporary separation from their true cosmic nature. They seek methods of recovering the awareness lost when the Drift shaped the first bodies.

The phrase **“the infinite cannot fit inside a person”** appears throughout Moyishuu teaching. Some use it to encourage humility regarding cosmic dreams. Others use it to argue that death releases the mind from a body too small to contain its original nature.

Star Forges occupy the center of many Moyishuu spiritual traditions. The dreams show regions where cosmic matter gathers and new stars emerge. Astronomers identify several physical stellar nurseries that resemble these visions, though no expedition has confirmed the extraordinary structures described in the oldest accounts.

Pilgrims travel toward nebulae, protostars, supernova remnants, and regions of unusual Drift activity in search of a true Star Forge. Some expect scientific discovery. Some seek religious confirmation. Others want to spend their final years looking upon a place that resembles the dreams they carried throughout life.

A small group of traditions teaches that a Moyishuu can surrender their physical body and return their consciousness to cosmic dust. Attempts usually involve prolonged exposure to vacuum, stellar radiation, Drift currents, or dangerous magical processes. Verified evidence of continued consciousness has never emerged from these practices.

Most constellations treat deliberate dissolution as death. Some still honor those who attempt it as pilgrims. Others regard the practice as the destruction of a finite person in pursuit of an existence that may never have contained individuality at all.

Dream preservation carries moral importance. Recording an experience allows it to remain available after the person’s memory changes or ends. Moyishuu preserve travel journals, performances, navigation records, objects, images, and spoken testimony with the same care given to inherited cosmic dreams.

This practice does not require permanent attachment to every object or memory. Many Moyishuu believe an experience can shape a person after its physical record is discarded. Others maintain enormous personal archives because they fear the Drift’s ability to erode sequence, detail, and certainty.

The Star Dream holds a complicated place in Moyishuu belief. Their traditions claim that the first formless awareness received it before any other Elect people. The first embodied Moyishuu entered recorded history after other civilizations had already begun using Drift technology, leaving the claim difficult to verify.

Erbium historians place Moyishuu reception later in the official chronology because the earliest accepted records concern embodied witnesses. Moyishuu scholars argue that requiring a body, government, and timestamp before recognizing an experience merely excludes forms of life that existed outside Settled Systems assumptions.

Recognition as the first recipients would give Moyishuu claims considerable religious and political weight. Many Moyishuu care more about recovering the truth than acquiring authority from it. Others believe official recognition could weaken Erbium’s claim to act as the primary steward of the Star Dream.

**Popular Anathema** prevent another person from leaving solely to preserve your comfort, falsify a dream record to claim spiritual authority, treat a temporary commitment as insincere, deliberately strand a dependent community, destroy another person’s records to control their identity, force someone toward cosmic dissolution

# Sample Names

Common Moyishuu names include Ayul, Dealo, Eshara, Kissarim, Lume, Nacre, Pearl, Rhyme, Senn, Vaio, Wildwood, Xori, Zera, and ZZZZZZZ.

Moyishuu names are chosen for sound, personal association, visual appearance when written, or the circumstances surrounding a child’s birth. Parents often select unusual sounds heard through a ship, instrument, transmission, or dream.

A Moyishuu may adopt an additional name after a major journey or experience. Examples include **Ayul Red Passage**, **Pearl of the Glass Wake**, **Senn Three-Suns**, **Wildwood Under Pressure**, and **Dealo of the Returning Line**.

Constellation affiliation can appear as part of a formal name. Examples include **Kissarim of Lantern Array**, **Lume aboard Patient Horizon**, and **Xori of the Seven Near Stars**.

# Other Information

## Constellations

A constellation provides family, transport, housing, education, medical support, and access to the Drift. Its name may refer to its founding ship, a route, an inherited dream, a stellar formation, or an event remembered by its members.

Examples include **Lantern Array**, **Patient Horizon**, **The Seven Near Stars**, **Blue Dust Returning**, **The Open Wake**, and **A Place Between Signals**.

Some constellations own several large vessels and negotiate directly with governments. Others consist of one aging ship and a few families working enough contracts to keep it operational.

Members can leave a constellation, though departure may require settling shared debts, transferring care responsibilities, returning communal equipment, or arranging passage away from the current route.

A constellation that loses its engine remains capable of entering the Drift through a Moyishuu Eikon. The resulting voyage can take months or years using conventional propulsion. Many stranded constellations accept Erbium assistance because the alternative would separate families and delay travel for a substantial portion of their lives.

## Conception in the Drift

Moyishuu conception requires Drift exposure. Their reproductive cells become fully viable only while immersed in the plane’s energy.

The prospective parents must be physically present within the Drift. Conception may occur naturally or through assisted reproductive medicine. Once fertilization is complete, development proceeds normally in either hyperspace or realspace.

Constellations maintain private medical chambers for conception, fertility care, and early pregnancy. These rooms use shielded walls, adjustable gravity, medical monitoring, and emergency stabilization equipment.

Traditional voyages involve the parents entering the Drift with close family and trusted members of their constellation. Larger communities schedule several families during the same passage so the cost and risk can be shared.

Settled Systems medical providers call these journeys **reproductive Drift exposures**. Moyishuu usually call them **beginning voyages**.

Erbium offers subsidized beginning voyages to employees and registered dependents. Benefits may include medical screening, scheduled Drift access, embryo care, private accommodation, and priority treatment during complications.

Eligibility depends on employment status, approved vessel access, medical authorization, route availability, and continued participation in the relevant benefit program. Loss of employment during treatment can transfer the cost of the voyage to the family or constellation.

Independent Moyishuu communities use Drift Eikons to conduct beginning voyages without an engine. These crossings require a starship, conventional propulsion, fuel, medical support, and a safe location from which to enter and leave the Drift.

Erbium describes unregistered conception crossings as a reproductive safety concern. Moyishuu activists argue that corporate licensing converts the continuation of their ancestry into an employment benefit.

## The Four Courts

The Moyishuu Courts are cultural traditions describing how an individual constructs a meaningful life within the Second Dream. They function as schools, social networks, artistic movements, and practical philosophies.

A Moyishuu may study several Courts, though childhood instruction and constellation culture usually emphasize one.

### Court of Dance

The Court of Dance values movement, bodily experience, improvisation, travel, athletic practice, and the ability to respond to changing circumstances.

Its members frequently work as pilots, acrobats, messengers, athletes, emergency crew, performers, and guides. Dance traditions include formal choreography, zero-gravity movement, tether work, and practical exercises for navigating damaged vessels.

### Court of Fields

The Court of Fields values sustained effort, cultivation, diplomacy, settlement, repair, and commitments whose results emerge slowly.

Its name refers to both agricultural fields and the gravitational, magnetic, and social fields through which living systems develop. Members commonly become growers, engineers, negotiators, teachers, planners, caregivers, and organizers of long voyages.

### Court of Flames

The Court of Flames values direct action, confrontation, urgency, competition, and deliberate change.

Its members often become security personnel, rescuers, activists, soldiers, competitive performers, mechanics, and people willing to make decisions during crisis. The Court teaches that decisive action carries responsibility for every consequence it creates.

### Court of Song

The Court of Song values communication, memory, teaching, art, testimony, and the preservation of experience.

Its members include musicians, historians, broadcasters, legal witnesses, archivists, scientists, entertainers, and translators. Song can refer to any patterned expression capable of carrying experience from one person to another.

The Courts create stereotypes. Dancers are treated as unreliable, Fields as cautious, Flames as reckless, and Singers as attention-seeking. Moyishuu frequently use the same assumptions while criticizing outsiders for doing so.

## Moyish and Driftsong

**Moyish** is the native language of the Moyishuu. Its spoken form combines rapid shifts in rhythm, extended vowels, repeated consonants, changes in volume, and sounds produced by tapping or resonating the crystalline hair.

Written Moyish uses clustered symbols whose spacing and orientation describe timing. A sentence can be read linearly, though its layout may also indicate emphasis, interruption, and simultaneous ideas.

**Driftsong** is a specialized communication register used aboard ships and during vacuum work. It combines radio tones, transmitted light, hull vibration, tactile rhythm, and abbreviated Moyish speech.

Driftsong remains functional during many communications failures. Crew members can tap messages through bulkheads, transmit patterns through suit lamps, or resonate crystal against structural panels.

Other ancestries can learn Moyish normally. Driftsong requires specialized training and compatible equipment for communication outside physical contact.

## Star Forges

Star Forges appear throughout Moyishuu inherited dreams as immense regions where cosmic material collapses, ignites, and organizes into stars.

Some resemble known stellar nurseries. Others include impossible structures, repeated patterns, or concentrations of matter inconsistent with ordinary astronomy. Different Moyishuu can dream of the same Forge across several generations.

Constellations compare these accounts with surveys of nebulae, protostellar disks, stellar remnants, and unusual Drift currents. Several expeditions have found locations sharing portions of a recorded dream, though none has confirmed a complete match.

Erbium funds Star Forge research when it may reveal new energy sources, navigation routes, rare elements, or Drift phenomena. Contracts typically assign all survey data, biological samples, stellar measurements, and discoveries to the company.

Moyishuu researchers frequently conceal portions of their dream archives from corporate sponsors. Some fear exploitation of the Star Forges. Others believe Erbium would suppress evidence supporting Moyishuu claims about the first reception of the Star Dream.

## Place in the Settled Systems

Moyishuu are distributed throughout the Settled Systems rather than concentrated on a single planet. Large populations live aboard constellation ships, major ports, relay stations, Drift research facilities, and settlements positioned along busy transit routes.

They are heavily recruited as pilots, navigators, scouts, performers, diplomats, emergency transit specialists, stellar researchers, couriers, surveyors, and rescue personnel.

Drift Eikon makes every Moyishuu a potential continuity resource. Erbium vessel policies commonly require Moyishuu crew members to register the ability, complete emergency-entry training, and remain available when a ship’s ordinary engine becomes inoperable.

A Moyishuu employee may be listed as an auxiliary propulsion asset even when hired for an unrelated position. Employment contracts can require participation in emergency navigation drills, restrict unsupervised Drift entry, and assign liability for vessels carried into unauthorized routes.

The ability does not replace commercial Drift engines. A vessel entering through an Eikon moves using conventional thrusters and may require an impractical amount of time to cross interstellar distances. Erbium uses this limitation to describe Eikons as emergency safeguards rather than competitors.

Independent settlements value them for the same reason. Slow access remains preferable to complete isolation. A Moyishuu pilot can evacuate a community after Erbium suspends service, guide a vessel toward a nearby current, or establish a temporary connection for a beginning voyage.

Erbium research divisions study Moyishuu crystalline tissue, neurological activity, vacuum adaptation, and interaction with Drift boundaries. The company publicly describes this work as an effort to improve medical care and emergency navigation.

Moyishuu communities suspect the ultimate objective is a synthetic Eikon capable of opening the Drift without a living pilot. Such a device would allow Erbium to retain the ancestry’s biological advantage while removing the person capable of refusing service.

Moyishuu serving in the Frontier Extraction Division commonly work as pilots, scouts, survey personnel, stellar observers, communications specialists, performers, navigators, and emergency transportation assets.

Some enter frontier service because it provides access to unfamiliar systems and sights few settled-world residents ever experience. Others use corporate passage to search for Star Forges, support a struggling constellation, conduct a beginning voyage, or reach a place Erbium never intended them to find.
