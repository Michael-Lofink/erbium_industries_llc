# Quantum

A quantum object or environmental feature has several possible configurations. Observation holds it in its current configuration.
When observation ends, it shifts according to its entry.

Each feature defines its extent, possible configurations, and the order or method used to select its next configuration.

## Observation

A creature watching a recognizable part of a quantum feature stabilizes the entire feature. The observer must have sufficient light or an appropriate visual sense, such as darkvision. Artificial vision counts. Companions and drones can stabilize a feature while actively watching it through their visual sensors.

Observation persists through ordinary blinking. It ends when the observer deliberately looks away, closes their eyes, loses visibility, or becomes unconscious.

Observation can pass between observers as long as someone maintains sight throughout the handoff.

## Shifting

When the final observer loses sight of the feature, it shifts instantaneously to its next configuration. Observing it again allows another shift when that observation ends.

Creatures and unrelated solid objects must fit safely within the resulting arrangement. If the destination is obstructed, the feature remains in place until another observation-and-release cycle allows a new attempt.

Any dangerous effects of shifting are described in the feature's entry.

## Quantum Images

Watching a clear photograph or live visual feed of a quantum feature stabilizes it as direct observation would.

A photograph must depict the feature's current configuration and have been captured while it occupied that configuration.
It remains effective until the feature shifts, after which a fresh photograph is required.

Observers can hand off observation between direct sight and a valid image.

## Entanglement

Creatures and loose objects entirely inside a quantum chamber, or entirely supported by a quantum platform, travel with it. Worn and carried equipment accompanies them.

Occupants who can see the feature stabilize it. Traveling with a shift requires every observer, including those watching images, to release observation.

A creature straddling the feature's boundary holds it in place. A secured tether connecting the feature or its occupants to fixed surroundings also holds it in place until disconnected.

## During Play

During exploration, a declared watcher maintains observation while visibility and their activities allow it.

During encounters, each observer can maintain watch over one quantum feature at a time as part of their normal actions.
Movement and other activities can continue while the observer maintains the necessary view.

Before a voluntary action breaks declared observation, the GM tells the player that consequence.
