Brenneri trace their ancestry to the coastal shelves, floating districts, and submerged settlements of [[Wander]]. Their large eyes, sensitive hearing, expressive whiskers, and constant awareness of nearby emotions have given them a reputation for friendliness, caution, and social anxiety. Brenneri can often sense a conflict developing before anyone speaks, though sensing that something is wrong does not necessarily tell them why.

During early development, most brenneri become attached to an object such as a smooth stone or worn tool component. Known as an **anchor**, its familiar texture and weight help them distinguish their own emotions from those they sense nearby.

Erbium Industries classifies anchors as **affective stabilization articles**. Under Settled Systems Code `A4%7&6-2`, employers may place an anchor into secure custodial retention for the duration of designated service. The policy is presented as a way to protect irreplaceable objects from workplace hazards, prevent emotional dependency, and encourage independent regulation. In practice, access to an anchor is often used as a performance incentive, disciplinary tool, and guarantee that a brenneri will complete their assigned service.

Brenneri are frequently recruited into mediation and **Ancestry Resources**. Erbium teaches them to turn their perception of distress and changing group morale into assessments for management.

A brenneri might spend their career calming workers whose problems they have no authority to solve. Those who eventually receive their anchors back often do so late in life, after fulfilling a service obligation that defined most of their adulthood.

Brenneri characters often balance concern for distressed people with pressure from employers to monitor those same people.

# Physical Description

Brenneris are bipedal, otter-like humanoids who average approximately 5 feet in height and usually possess slight, compact builds. Their short, dense fur can vary considerably in color, though shades of brown, gray, cream, black, rust, and muted gold are common. Many have lighter fur along the muzzle, throat, chest, abdomen, or inner limbs.

Their webbed hands and feet end in short claws suited to swimming, climbing wet surfaces, and handling objects. A thick, muscular tail provides propulsion and balance in water. Brenneris can hold their breath for extended periods, but they are air-breathing mammals and cannot survive indefinitely underwater without environmental equipment.

Large, round eyes allow brenneris to detect movement and danger under poor lighting conditions. Their hearing extends beyond the ordinary range of many other ancestries, allowing them to perceive distant machinery, structural vibration, infrasonic calls, and subtle changes in breathing or speech.

A brenneri’s whiskers respond to physical changes associated with emotion, including pheromones and electromagnetic activity. Strong distress can be difficult to ignore. These sensations reveal emotional states, with their causes left for the brenneri to interpret.

This sensitivity frequently produces hypervigilance. Brenneris monitor tone, posture, breathing, movement, and environmental changes almost constantly. Many use deliberate breathing exercises, repetitive movements, touch, familiar scents, or a bonded anchor to distinguish their own emotional state from those around them.

Brenneris possess naturally water-resistant pouches beneath their arms. Historically, these protected food, small tools, shells, and anchors from saltwater and rough currents. Modern workwear often incorporates access panels or protective compartments around these pouches, although standardized Erbium uniforms do not always accommodate them comfortably.

# Society

Traditional brenneri communities form **rafts**: small groups of relatives and trusted companions who share practical care and emotional support.

The term comes from a survival practice on flooded Wander. Brenneris linked limbs and tails while floating to support children and exhausted swimmers. Modern rafts continue to organize mutual aid wherever their members live.

A raft is rarely organized around one permanent leader. Different members take responsibility according to their skills and condition. A brenneri who provides emotional support during one crisis may depend completely on the same community during another. This expectation of reciprocal care can clash sharply with Settled Systems institutions, which assign responsibility according to employment title, eligibility, and contractual obligation.

Children develop an anchor’s reassuring associations through repeated use during distress or separation from caregivers. Familiarity determines its value to them.

The destruction of Wander intensified the cultural importance of these objects. Many surviving anchors are among the few personal items carried away from lost communities. A chipped stone or mineral-smoothed machine bearing may be an ordinary object to everyone else and the last surviving physical connection to a brenneri’s family district.

Brenneri etiquette requires consent before questioning someone about sensed distress or sharing that information. They are expected to acknowledge uncertainty about its cause.

Corporate training requires brenneris to record emotional irregularities and report perceived risks to management. Ancestry Resources assigns them to sensitive work such as grievance intake and disciplinary meetings.

Some brenneris consider these occupations a legitimate extension of their cultural responsibility to reduce harm. Others argue that Erbium uses their empathy to make exploitation more tolerable without addressing its causes. A brenneri mediator may be praised for calming an angry extraction crew while the unsafe quota that caused the anger remains unchanged.

Brenneris outside corporate systems maintain anchor exchanges, emotional-support networks, and informal rafts for workers separated from their communities. Some conceal anchors that were supposed to be surrendered at the beginning of service. Others maintain counterfeit custody records or arrange brief unauthorized reunions between workers and the objects Erbium holds over them.

# Beliefs

The raft appears throughout brenneri religious and civic traditions. These traditions often judge communities by the care they provide during hardship.

Many brenneris interpret **the Star Dream** as the largest shared emotional experience in known history. Peoples on distant worlds received the same revelation and were moved to seek one another despite having no prior relationship. To some brenneris, this suggests that connection is a fundamental property of sapient life. Others reject that interpretation, particularly when the doctrine of the Elect is used to deny dignity to those who never experienced the Dream.

Brenneri ethics frequently distinguish between recognizing an emotion and claiming authority over it. Their senses may reveal that someone is afraid, furious, or grieving, but the person experiencing that emotion retains the right to define its meaning.

Anchor custody has become a major religious and cultural dispute. Erbium-approved authorities argue that temporary separation develops resilience and protects valuable objects. Many brenneris consider involuntary separation a deliberate disruption of a formative bond. Destroying, withholding, or threatening another person’s anchor is widely treated as an act of intimate coercion.

**Popular Edicts** keep vulnerable people afloat, listen before interpreting another person’s emotions, protect another person’s anchor, de-escalate conflict without concealing its cause, contribute to the raft when you are able

**Popular Anathema** weaponize knowledge of another person’s emotions, destroy or withhold an anchor to compel obedience, force someone to mediate a conflict, abandon a dependent during danger, claim certainty about feelings you have only partially sensed

# Other Information

## Anchor Custody

Settled Systems Code `A4%7&6-2`, formally titled the **Personal Stabilization Article Custodial Accommodation**, permits designated employers and civic-service authorities to retain objects identified as necessary to an employee’s emotional regulation.

The provision is ancestry-neutral, but it is applied most extensively to brenneri anchors.

Erbium presents custody as protection against damage or loss and as training in independent emotional regulation.

At the beginning of designated service, a brenneri’s anchor is catalogued, scanned, assigned an asset-storage record, and transferred to an approved retention facility. The brenneri may receive a visual replica, material substitute, or digital record of the object. These replacements rarely reproduce the accumulated sensory familiarity of the original.

Management can grant supervised access for clinical care or as a service incentive.

The anchor is scheduled for return when the brenneri completes every qualifying service obligation. Outstanding equipment debt, unresolved housing charges, continuity disputes, civic-service assignments, and disciplinary reviews can delay release.

When a brenneri dies before release, the anchor is supposed to pass to a designated raft member or beneficiary. Storage fees, inheritance disputes, and corporate property claims frequently prevent that transfer.

> [!quote] Erbium Employee Wellness Services
> Your anchor will remain in an approved retention facility for the duration of your service.

## Ancestry Resources

**Ancestry Resources** is the collection of Erbium departments responsible for workplace integration among different ancestries. Its stated responsibilities include accommodation, communication, conflict resolution, cultural orientation, accessibility, and employee welfare.

Brenneris are disproportionately assigned to grievance intake and labor mediation, where they record their interpretations of employee distress.

Management often treats these interpretations as objective evidence. A brenneri sensing fear may be pressed to report deception, or to classify anger during labor organizing as a security threat.

Workers may therefore distrust corporate brenneris even when the brenneri is attempting to help them. Some Ancestry Resources employees falsify assessments, omit private information, or classify justified anger as ordinary occupational stress to protect their coworkers.

## Rafts Beyond Wander

The loss of Wander scattered brenneri communities across the Settled Systems. Many modern rafts consist of unrelated workers, refugees, neighbors, and traveling companions who have agreed to provide mutual support.

A raft may pool housing, medical costs, legal representation, and transportation credits. Members may preserve one another’s anchors, maintain independent emotional records, or provide testimony when corporate assessments contradict a brenneri’s own account of their mental state.

Erbium recognizes some rafts as household units when doing so simplifies housing and benefit administration. The company can also classify a raft as an unlicensed financial association, unauthorized care provider, or source of coordinated workplace interference.

#### Darkvision
You can see in darkness and dim light just as well as you can see in bright light, though your vision in darkness is in black and white.

#### Empathetic Whiskers
Your whiskers continuously sense the emotions of those around you, which makes you susceptible to mirroring their feelings. You have a +2 circumstance bonus to Sense Motive checks regarding the emotional state of an adjacent creature. You take a –1 circumstance penalty to Will saves against [emotion](https://2e.aonsrd.com/traits/72-emotion) effects. When you end your turn adjacent to an ally with the [frightened](https://2e.aonsrd.com/conditions/18-frightened) condition, you must attempt a DC 5 flat check. On a failure, you become frightened 1.

You gain the [Breath Control](https://2e.aonsrd.com/feats/773-breath-control) general feat.
