# Creating a Settled Systems Code

> [!summary]
> Characters may refer to provisions of the [[Settled Systems Administrative Code]] in their backstories, employment records, criminal histories, licenses, contracts, medical files, military service, or family history.
>
> You do not need to write a complete law. Usually, you only need:
>
> 1. what the law regulates;
> 2. the relevant Administrative Title or chapter;
> 3. the public reason the law exists;
> 4. how it affected your character.
>
> Exact section and clause numbers should be confirmed by the GM before becoming setting canon.

## The Safest Way to Cite a Law

A complete citation uses this format:

`A[TITLE]%[CHAPTER]&[SECTION]-[CLAUSE]`

Example:

`A2%13&1-3`

This means:

- `A2` — Administrative Title 2
- `%13` — Chapter 13
- `&1` — Section 1
- `-3` — Clause 3

You may cite only the title or chapter when the exact provision has not been established.

Examples:

- **Administrative Title 4**
- **Title Four labor regulations**
- **Settled Systems Code `A4%5`**
- **the workplace-safety provisions of `A4%5`**
- **a passenger-eligibility rule under `A5%8`**

> [!important] Player-Safe Rule
> Using an established chapter stem without inventing a section or clause cannot conflict with an existing law.
>
> Write `A4%5` during character creation. The GM can assign the complete citation later if one is needed.

## Step 1: Decide What the Law Actually Does

Choose the law’s primary legal effect.

Ask which statement best describes it:

- It establishes who counts as a person.
- It verifies identity or maintains a record.
- It determines who owns something.
- It creates a debt or lease obligation.
- It regulates employment or compensation.
- It controls travel, cargo, docking, or relay use.
- It authorizes a search, detention, or security action.
- It regulates land, extraction, settlement, or environmental damage.
- It governs citizenship or government authority.
- It creates emergency powers or quarantine restrictions.

Use the title that governs this primary effect.

A law can interact with several titles. Choose one controlling title and treat the others as related provisions.

### Example

A company can repossess a leased cybernetic limb.

- The ownership and lease rule belongs under `A3`.
- A security provision authorizing forced entry would belong under `A6`.
- A medical-consent dispute might involve `A2`.

The backstory can mention all three without assigning three complete citations.

## Step 2: Give the Law a Formal Name

Settled Systems laws usually have calm, administrative names.

Useful terms include:

- Continuity
- Eligibility
- Verification
- Allocation
- Responsible Use
- Public Safety
- Service
- Infrastructure
- Authentication
- Preservation
- Interoperability
- Stabilization
- Recognition
- Custodial
- Transitional
- Occupational
- Emergency

### Example Names

- **Dependent Housing Transitional Eligibility Standard**
- **Certified Equipment Custodial Responsibility Provision**
- **Passenger Continuity Verification Requirement**
- **Occupational Exposure Reporting Standard**
- **Emergency Infrastructure Service Allocation**
- **Recognized Settlement Water-Use Provision**

These are examples of naming style. They are not established laws.

## Step 3: Give It a Reasonable Public Purpose

Most laws claim to address a real administrative or safety problem.

Examples include:

- preventing identity theft;
- protecting life-support infrastructure;
- keeping navigation records compatible;
- ensuring hazardous equipment receives maintenance;
- preserving access to emergency housing;
- preventing contamination;
- coordinating evacuations;
- establishing financial responsibility;
- keeping dangerous cargo off passenger vessels;
- determining which government has jurisdiction.

A law can serve a legitimate purpose and still harm people through cost, access, enforcement, or exemptions.

## Step 4: Decide How It Affected Your Character

Useful backstory consequences include:

- Your family inherited an obligation attached to property or equipment.
- You lost housing when a parent’s employment ended.
- A record error made you ineligible for travel.
- Your medical procedure required corporate authorization.
- You were classified as an independent contractor and denied employee benefits.
- Your settlement lost a land, water, or extraction claim.
- Your identity was placed under review.
- You performed emergency or civic service.
- Your ship was detained over a registry discrepancy.
- You were blamed for equipment certified by someone else.
- You received protection through a law that others regularly abuse.
- Your community relied on an exemption that was later revoked.
- A local official helped you appeal an unfair application.
- Your employer followed the law exactly and still left you with the cost.

## Step 5: Leave the Exact Number Open

Section and clause numbers are permanent registry assignments. They do not identify the law’s date, severity, ancestry, penalty, or author.

Do not choose a number because it looks meaningful.

During character creation, write:

> My family lost its housing under a transitional eligibility provision in Settled Systems Code `A4%7`. The exact clause is pending GM confirmation.

Or:

> My character was detained under an identity-verification provision of `A6%4`.

Or:

> The accident was governed by workplace-safety and assumed-risk rules under `A4%5`.

The GM can assign the complete citation after checking the current Code Register.

## Writing Style

A Settled Systems law usually sounds calm, technical, and impersonal.

### Appropriate

> The provision establishes continued habitation eligibility during a verified period of employment separation.

> Certified operators must preserve inspection records needed to establish responsible maintenance.

> Passenger access may be suspended until continuity verification is restored.

### Avoid

> Erbium can throw workers into space whenever it wants.

> This law lets the corporation steal your body.

> Anyone who breaks this rule disappears forever.

The practical result may resemble those statements. The official wording should describe safety, continuity, eligibility, verification, infrastructure protection, or responsible administration.

## Quick Backstory Template

Copy and complete this section:

### Proposed Backstory Law

**What happened to my character or their family?**  
[One or two sentences.]

**What does the law regulate?**  
[Employment, identity, property, travel, security, settlement, emergency powers, etc.]

**Primary legal effect:**  
[What the law actually requires, permits, recognizes, or prohibits.]

**Suggested title or chapter:**  
[`A#` or `A#%#`]

**Proposed formal name:**  
[Technical and institutionally neutral title.]

**Public purpose:**  
[Why authorities say the law is necessary.]

**Practical effect:**  
[What happened to ordinary people or to the character.]

**Who administered it?**  
[Employer, local government, registry, port authority, court, medical provider, security office, etc.]

**Possible exception or appeal:**  
[Optional.]

**Exact citation:**  
[Pending GM confirmation.]

## Compact Example

> My mother worked as a pressure technician aboard an Erbium-operated habitat. After an injury ended her employment, our family received only a short period to transfer our housing eligibility.
>
> **Suggested chapter:** `A4%7` — Benefits, Housing, and Service Eligibility  
> **Proposed law:** *Pressure-Rated Housing Transitional Eligibility Standard*  
> **Public purpose:** Ensuring every occupied unit has a responsible operator and funded life-support service  
> **Practical effect:** Our family had thirty days to find a new sponsor, employer, or residence  
> **Exact citation:** Pending GM confirmation

> [!tip]
> A good backstory law creates a specific pressure, obligation, protection, dispute, or relationship.
>
> It should give your character something to care about during play: a debt, an appeal, a missing record, a former supervisor, a protected relative, an expired exemption, a legal enemy, or someone who once helped them.
