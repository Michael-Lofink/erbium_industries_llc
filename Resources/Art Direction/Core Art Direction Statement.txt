Core Art Direction Statement

Corporate Frontier Grit is a grounded, low-science-fiction visual style built around blue-collar extraction work at the edge of colonized space. It combines utilitarian industrial hardware, modular frontier infrastructure, controlled corporate branding, and an undercurrent of cosmic unease. Technology is advanced but not elegant. Surfaces are worn, patched, dented, dust-coated, and branded. The world should feel like humanity expanded into space with contract labor, leased equipment, prefab structures, and PR-approved optimism, not with utopian abundance.

# Corporate Frontier Grit

> [!summary]
> A grounded, low-science-fiction visual style centered on blue-collar extraction work at the edge of settled space. Advanced technology is practical, modular, expensive, and frequently repaired. Corporate branding remains clean and reassuring while workers, equipment, and frontier facilities show the physical cost of expansion.

## Core Visual Direction

**Rendering:** Stylized concept-art realism with clear silhouettes, believable materials, restrained saturation, and moderate surface detail.

**World tone:** Industrial competence, institutional neglect, corporate optimism, labor exploitation, frontier isolation, and restrained cosmic unease.

**Technology:** Advanced without appearing effortless. Machines require fuel, maintenance, replacement parts, operators, cables, seals, vents, and access panels.

**Condition:** Worn but functional. Use chipped paint, dust, patched panels, grease near moving parts, faded labels, replacement components, and repairs made with available materials.

## Visual Pillars

### Industrial Practicality

Every object should communicate its function through visible construction.

- Reinforced corners, handles, hinges, bolts, clamps, vents, and maintenance panels
- Modular components designed for repair and replacement
- Heavy equipment with readable weight and mechanical purpose
- Equipment labels, serial numbers, hazard markings, and inspection tags

### Corporate Reassurance

Erbium Industries presents extraction as orderly, safe, scientific, and necessary.

- Clean geometric branding applied to damaged or dangerous equipment
- Polished recruitment material contrasted with harsh working conditions
- Standardized uniforms, signage, packaging, interfaces, and safety language
- Euphemistic slogans and legal disclaimers

### Frontier Labor

Settlements and worksites are built quickly, expanded unevenly, and maintained under limited budgets.

- Prefabricated structures, cargo modules, gantries, tanks, floodlights, and temporary barriers
- Crowded barracks, utilitarian mess halls, equipment lockers, and improvised repairs
- Workers who appear capable, tired, practical, and visibly employed
- Limited personal decoration inside standardized corporate spaces

### Cosmic Unknown

Unsettled systems contain phenomena that resist easy classification without becoming overt fantasy.

- Strange geology, unusual erosion, unfamiliar biology, and anomalous light behavior
- Sparse visual evidence rather than constant spectacle
- Scientific equipment used to study things it cannot fully explain
- Anomalies should feel physically present and dangerous

## Shape Language

**Erbium Industries:** Faceted polygons, chamfered rectangles, trapezoids, hexagonal structures, inset panels, hard diagonals, and controlled symmetry.

**Frontier infrastructure:** Boxy modules, cylindrical tanks, exposed frames, stacked containers, braces, ladders, gantries, and service platforms.

**Natural environments:** Fractured stone, layered strata, mineral shear planes, irregular caverns, asymmetrical formations, and weathered surfaces.

**Unknown technology or structures:** Precise but unfamiliar geometry, limited ornament, ambiguous function, and forms that do not resemble conventional human manufacturing.

## Materials and Surfaces

Primary materials:

- Painted steel and dull aluminum
- Ceramic composite panels
- Matte polymers and rubber seals
- Reinforced glass and scratched visors
- Canvas, webbing, pressure fabric, and insulated padding
- Carbon-black equipment housings

Preferred wear:

- Scratched edges
- Chipped paint
- Dust collected in seams
- Heat discoloration
- Oil residue around mechanical joints
- Faded stencils
- Mismatched replacement panels

Avoid universal rust, excessive grime, mirror chrome, seamless plastics, and pristine showroom finishes.

## Color Palette

### Erbium Industries

- Dark charcoal
- Gunmetal
- Cold silver
- Pale gray
- Muted erbium-magenta or violet-pink

Magenta is a controlled identification color used for logos, interface highlights, uniform piping, division markings, equipment labels, and status indicators.

### Frontier Environments

- Weathered off-white
- Gray-blue steel
- Faded industrial yellow
- Dusty orange-brown
- Olive drab
- Dirty beige
- Desaturated hazard red

### Anomalies

Use sparingly:

- Pale cyan
- Deep indigo
- Muted ultraviolet
- Spectral green
- Pearlescent white
- Dim amber

Avoid saturated rainbow lighting and dominant cyberpunk neon.

## Lighting

Use practical sources:

- Industrial floodlights
- Overhead work strips
- Vehicle lamps
- Helmet and shoulder lights
- Emergency beacons
- Sodium-yellow facility lighting
- Cold planetary or stellar light
- Equipment screens and status indicators

Lighting should reveal dust, depth, machinery, damage, and working conditions. Corporate interiors may be cleaner and more evenly illuminated than frontier installations.

## Character Design

Workers should resemble miners, technicians, surveyors, pilots, logistics personnel, security contractors, and maintenance crews.

Common elements:

- Utility jumpsuits and layered pressure clothing
- Reinforced boots and gloves
- Tool harnesses and tether points
- ID patches, serial markings, and company insignia
- Practical helmets, lamps, air systems, and equipment packs
- Visible wear, repairs, and personalized adjustments

Corporate officials use cleaner materials, better tailoring, fewer tools, and more controlled branding. Security equipment should feel commercially manufactured and workmanlike rather than ceremonial.

## Erbium Industries Brand Rules

The Erbium emblem is the cleanest visual element in the setting.

- Use charcoal, pale gray, silver, and muted magenta
- Maintain faceted mineral geometry
- Use the **Er** monogram and **68** designation consistently
- Apply the detailed logo to recruitment material, headquarters, and formal displays
- Apply the simplified emblem to uniforms, machinery, crates, helmets, doors, and ship hulls
- Keep typography bold, technical, geometric, and highly legible

The anti-corporate tone comes from where the branding appears: malfunctioning equipment, leased survival tools, hazardous worksites, employee contracts, and memorial records.

## Avoid

- Sleek utopian futurism
- Excessive cyberpunk neon
- Cartoon proportions
- Retro-futurist comedy as the default tone
- Fantasy crystals and magical visual effects
- Decorative technology without a visible function
- Universally filthy or ruined environments
- Smooth luxury spacecraft used as ordinary work vehicles
- Generic military styling for all corporate personnel

# Creature Template

> Design a native creature from **[planet, moon, habitat, or system]** known locally as **[creature name or provisional designation]**.
>
> The creature occupies the ecological role of **[predator, scavenger, grazer, filter feeder, burrower, parasite, ambush hunter, colony organism, or symbiotic species]**. It survives in **[gravity, atmospheric pressure, temperature, radiation, terrain, available food, and other environmental conditions]**.
>
> Build its anatomy around practical adaptations:
>
> - **Locomotion:** [legs, gripping limbs, fins, membrane wings, burrowing plates, low-gravity propulsion, or anchored movement]
> - **Feeding:** [jaw structure, filtering organ, mineral scraping surface, proboscis, crushing plates, chemical digestion]
> - **Senses:** [heat detection, vibration sensing, polarized vision, pressure sensitivity, electrical fields, scent organs]
> - **Protection:** [shell, dense hide, flexible armor, camouflage, warning coloration, defensive secretion, rapid retreat]
> - **Reproduction or colony behavior:** [eggs, spores, live young, budding, mobile larvae, hive structure, seasonal migration]
>
> Its anatomy should follow a coherent biological plan rather than combining unrelated animal features. The creature may look unfamiliar, but every major structure should support survival or behavior.
>
> Use **[natural colors derived from the habitat]** with **[limited warning coloration, reflective tissue, mineral deposits, or dim bioluminescence]** only where biologically useful. Avoid bright fantasy coloration unless the environment supports it.
>
> Show evidence of how workers encounter it: **[ore dust on its hide, damage from industrial equipment, nesting in warm machinery, feeding on waste, following survey lights, attacking vibration sources, or avoiding corporate deterrent markers]**.
>
> **Presentation:** creature concept sheet showing **[full-body side view, three-quarter view, scale comparison, head detail, movement study, or habitat vignette]**. Use a plain background for reference art or a restrained environmental background for ecological context.
>
> [Shared Style Block]

### Creature Tone Options

- **Common frontier wildlife:** practical, recognizable behavior, little cosmic mystery
- **Industrial pest:** attracted to heat, waste, radiation, wiring, or vibration
- **Large territorial animal:** dangerous due to behavior and environment rather than malice
- **Colony organism:** multiple bodies functioning as a shared biological system
- **Anomalous organism:** coherent anatomy with one scientifically troubling property
- **Corporate experiment:** visible intervention, tagging, implants, surgical alteration, or containment history

### Creature Negative Additions

> No fantasy dragon anatomy, no demonic symbolism, no random tentacles, no human skull face, no ornamental spikes without biological purpose, no magical aura, no excessive bioluminescence, no exact resemblance to a single Earth animal, no cute mascot styling unless requested

# Ship Template

> Design a **[ship class and role]** operated by **[Erbium Industries, independent workers, settlement authority, rival corporation, smugglers, or unknown faction]**.
>
> The vessel is built primarily for **[ore transport, survey work, personnel movement, drilling support, salvage, medical evacuation, security patrol, long-range expedition, or frontier resupply]**. Its silhouette should prioritize that role over elegance.
>
> Include visible functional systems:
>
> - **Propulsion:** [main drives, maneuvering thrusters, heat-management structures, fuel access]
> - **Cargo:** [external containers, internal hold, ore hoppers, loading doors, cranes, docking clamps]
> - **Crew access:** [airlocks, docking collar, boarding ramp, maintenance ladder, emergency hatch]
> - **Landing or docking:** [heavy landing gear, magnetic clamps, modular docking frame, asteroid anchors]
> - **Operations:** [sensor mast, drilling assembly, survey arrays, work lights, repair arms, communications equipment]
> - **Maintenance:** visible access panels, replaceable modules, service corridors, numbered components
>
> The construction uses boxy modules, cylindrical tanks, reinforced frames, chamfered hull plates, protective cages, exposed service structures, and practical radiator or heat-dissipation surfaces. Avoid smooth aerodynamic shaping unless atmospheric operation requires it.
>
> Show **[age and condition]** through faded paint, repaired hull panels, thruster staining, impact marks, replaced modules, cargo wear, or carefully maintained corporate surfaces.
>
> Apply **[Erbium Industries / Frontier Extraction Division / independent / removed corporate]** markings. Erbium vessels use charcoal, pale gray, cold silver, and restrained muted magenta identification panels. Include simple registration numbers and hazard markings without relying on long generated text.
>
> **Scale:** [small two-person utility craft / six-person survey ship / medium ore hauler / large mobile refinery / corporate transport].
>
> **Presentation:** isolated ship concept art, complete vessel visible, **[three-quarter exterior view / side orthographic / top-down view / landing configuration / landed on plain industrial pad]**, neutral background or minimal space backdrop.
>

### Ship Role Modifiers

- **Survey ship:** sensor arrays, sample storage, deployable probes, limited cargo capacity
- **Mining tender:** industrial arms, tool mounts, ore handling, worker airlocks
- **Ore hauler:** reinforced cargo spine, detachable containers, slow heavy silhouette
- **Frontier shuttle:** rugged landing gear, passenger compartment, external baggage modules
- **Salvage vessel:** cutting equipment, clamps, floodlights, irregular replacement parts
- **Corporate executive transport:** cleaner surfaces, concealed machinery, controlled branding
- **Stranded campaign ship:** practical range, repairable modules, visible weak points, enough interior space for recurring scenes

### Ship Negative Additions

> No sleek fighter-jet silhouette unless atmospheric, no luxury-yacht curves, no excessive weapon mounts, no meaningless wings in vacuum, no glowing energy sails unless specified, no impossible engine placement, no tiny landing gear beneath a massive hull, no decorative greeble covering every surface

# Consistency Checklist

Before generating, specify:

- What is its function?
- Who manufactured, owns, or maintains it?
- What environment was it designed for?
- What has happened to it since manufacture?
- Where does the Erbium branding appear?
- What parts move, open, detach, or require maintenance?
- What should remain readable at thumbnail size?
- Is the output concept art, a game asset, a portrait, an icon, or a scene?
- Should the background be transparent, neutral, or environmental?
- Which details must remain consistent across future images?
